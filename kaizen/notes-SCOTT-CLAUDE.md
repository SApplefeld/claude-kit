# Kaizen inbox: SCOTT-CLAUDE






- **A rule that defends one direction of a shared-surface race reads as defending both, and the undefended direction is the one that fails silently** (2026-08-28, coordinator seat, from a live sweep between two seats on one checkout). The doctrine's scope-and-safety bullet already treats the shared git index carefully: it names the two dangerous git semantics, mandates `git add <paths>` followed by a `git diff --cached --name-only` whose output is read as its own step before any pathspec-less commit, and says in as many words that the trap is symmetric, since your chain can sweep their staged files exactly as theirs can sweep yours. The trap is indeed symmetric. The DEFENSE is not, and nothing in the text says so. The staged-list read protects your commit from a peer's staged content, and it cannot protect your staged content from a peer's commit, because your read runs at your commit, which is after any peer's pathspec-less commit has already had its chance at your index entries. Ordering, not diligence: no care at your own commit closes a window that opened the moment you staged. What happened was ordinary and involved no rule-breaking by either seat. One seat staged a plan-doc hunk and spent a minute composing its commit message; the other committed its own plan-doc edit without a pathspec and carried both files. The signature is visible in the commit, two unrelated plan docs in one commit whose message describes only one of them. No content was lost, which is what makes it worth a note rather than an incident report: the loss was the commit MESSAGE, the reasoning for the change, which for a plan doc is not recoverable from the diff and is the artifact the next session actually reads. The general shape is the part worth carrying past git: WHERE A PROTECTION IS ONE-DIRECTIONAL BUT ITS RULE READS AS BIDIRECTIONAL, A SESSION THAT FOLLOWS THE RULE FAITHFULLY IS COVERED FOR HALF OF WHAT IT BELIEVES, and the uncovered half is usually the silent one, since here the staged content simply stops being staged with nothing announcing where it went. Directions worth weighing rather than a settled fix, and the third is the one this seat would pick. State the asymmetry in the doctrine bullet itself, one clause, so the rule stops over-promising, which is cheap and catches only the reader who was going to think about it anyway. Or give the index a claim marker the way the heavy-process budget is getting one in `claude-kit_seat-infrastructure_spec_v1.md` Section 3, which is real coverage and real ceremony for a window measured in seconds. Or move the mitigation to where the window is actually controlled, which is the STAGING end rather than the commit end: never let a file rest staged on a shared checkout, stage and commit as one uninterrupted step, or skip the index and commit by pathspec from the worktree. That last one costs nothing, needs no protocol, and is the only mitigation available to the session that stands to lose. Worth noting for whoever dispositions this that the seat infrastructure work is already building claim files for exactly this class of shared surface, so the question of whether the index joins that set is live rather than hypothetical, and the answer may well be no on ceremony-versus-window grounds.
- **A load check that enumerates processes is structurally blind to load that is not a process, and it returns clean rather than returning unknown** (2026-08-28, coordinator seat, claude-kit, from a slot grant that was wrong at the moment it was issued). The doctrine's heavy-process bullet already makes the right generalizing move once: it says `testhost`, `dotnet`, `node --test` and a build are instances rather than the boundary, and that the class is any foreign PROCESS holding the box's memory, its CPU, or the repo's binaries. That generalization stops one level short of where the kit now operates. An agent fan-out consumes the box and is not a foreign process at all: a subagent runs in-process inside its parent session, spawns no engine and adds no separately visible working set, so a peer session driving three reviewers presents the same shape in the process table as one sitting idle. The coordinator skill names machine-resource arbitration as one of its three functions and says nothing about this, so a seat following both documents exactly will clear a launch onto a loaded box with nothing in either document to warn it. Measured rather than hypothetical: one poll read zero dotnet, testhost, msbuild and vstest with 8 GB physical free, and at least five review-class agents were live across that same window, two belonging to one repo's worker and three to another's; the grant issued on that reading was caught only because the receiving session knew what the seat could not see and handed it back. The general shape worth carrying past this kit: WHERE A CHECK'S UNIT OF ACCOUNTING IS NOT THE UNIT OF CONSUMPTION, THE CHECK DOES NOT DEGRADE TO UNCERTAINTY, IT RETURNS A CONFIDENT FALSE NEGATIVE, which is worse than no check at all because a false negative is acted upon while an absent check is worked around. The repair cannot be better polling by the observer, because the information does not exist at the observer's layer; it has to be declaration by the consumer, each session stating when it dispatches subagents and when they return. Three directions for whoever dispositions this, and the second is the one this seat would pick. Add the clause to the doctrine bullet so its process framing stops over-promising, which is cheap and reaches only the reader already thinking about it. Or state it in the coordinator skill's arbitration function alongside an explicit declaration duty, which is where a seat actually looks at the moment it is about to grant, and which pairs with the claim-file work already specified in `claude-kit_seat-infrastructure_spec_v1.md` Section 3. Or leave both documents alone and treat the declaration duty as protocol each seat negotiates at run time, which is what happened here and did work, but only because every seat involved volunteered it unprompted, so it encodes nothing for the seat that does not.
- **Kaizen capture and handling belongs in the role definitions themselves, not only in the kaizen skill: the coordinator seat captures, dispositions and dispatches kaizen items, and every other seat routes them to the coordinator rather than actioning them inline or shelving them** (2026-08-28, operator instruction at the coordinator seat's keyboard, claude-kit). The operator's grant in substance: the coordinator holds full authority to record, commit, push, direct, plan and dispatch kaizen items raised to the claude-kit expert for handling by the claude-kit worker, and capturing friction and proactively resolving it is one of the main benefits of the seat. The doctrine's kaizen bullet today addresses whoever happens to notice the friction and tells them to propose a one-line note and jot it on a nod. That was written for a single session and it names no owner, which in a multi-seat fleet means capture is everyone's duty and therefore nobody's: a worker mid-plan weighs a note against its own leash and the note loses, an expert seat holding a commit freeze has no obvious moment to file one, and a note that does get written sits in an inbox no seat is accountable for clearing. The general shape worth carrying past kaizen: A RESPONSIBILITY THAT NAMES NO OWNER IS DISCHARGED BY WHICHEVER PARTY IS LEAST BUSY, WHICH IN A FLEET IS RELIABLY THE PARTY LEAST LIKELY TO HAVE SEEN THE THING, so the duty inverts against the evidence. Concretely this wants two clauses in the role definitions being authored in `claude-kit_seat-infrastructure_spec_v1.md` Section 3: the coordinator role gains kaizen capture, dispositioning and dispatch as a NAMED function carrying the standing operator authority, which also means the seat's closed set of three functions becomes four and the skill's own "the set is closed at three" sentence needs amending with it; and every other role gains the reciprocal routing duty, raise it to the coordinator and carry on, explicitly rather than by implication, since a role that is silent on kaizen leaves a worker to guess between filing, fixing and forgetting. Worth landing INSIDE Section 3 rather than as a later amendment, because that section is authoring the role definitions right now and a follow-up would be rework of a file that has not yet been committed once.
- **A positive control proves a check CAN SPEAK; it does not prove the check is AIMED AT THE RIGHT WORDS, and the doctrine mandates the first while reading as though it delivered the second** (2026-08-28, coordinator seat, claude-kit, from a sweep reported clean that was not, found by a fresh-context reviewer). The testing-discipline bullet is right that a check proving an absence must be run against a state known to hold the thing and watched to speak, and that where the subject cannot be put into that state a sibling that does hold it is the control. What it does not say, and what a careful reader will not infer, is that THE CONTROL MUST BE BUILT INDEPENDENTLY OF THE PATTERN. A control assembled from the same vocabulary assumption as the check inherits the check's blind spot exactly and then passes, which is worse than no control because it converts an untested assumption into evidence. The instance: a whole-tree sweep for a restated count used the pattern `three functions|closed at three`, ran a positive control, watched it speak, and reported the tree clean. A live payload document read `the machine-coordinator seat's three closed functions`, a spelling the pattern cannot match and the control was never going to contain, because the control had been drawn from what the pattern expected to find rather than from the space of things a real author might write. A reviewer found it with the variant `three closed`. THE GENERAL SHAPE WORTH CARRYING PAST GREP: A CONTROL DERIVED FROM THE INSTRUMENT TESTS THE INSTRUMENT'S MECHANISM AND NOT ITS COVERAGE, so it answers "does this fire" while the question was "does this fire on everything that matters", and the two are indistinguishable from the result. This reaches every absence-proving check the kit runs, not only sweeps: a hand-authored path list, a scoped grep whose acceptance is that it returns nothing, a sweep expected to come back clean. Two directions, and the second is the one this seat would pick. State the independence requirement as a clause on the existing bullet, which is cheap and reaches the reader already thinking about it. Or state the constructive form, which is stronger because it tells you what to DO rather than what to avoid: enumerate the subject space by a DIFFERENT METHOD than the pattern (find every occurrence of the number word and classify each, rather than searching for the phrasings you expect), and let the pattern be judged against that enumeration; where a different method is genuinely unavailable, the silence is unproven and says so. Worth noting for whoever dispositions this that the failure is self-concealing in the way the doctrine already warns about elsewhere, since a check gone quiet for the wrong reason reads exactly like a true clean result, and here the control is what made the wrong reason look adjudicated.
- **A cross-seat finding's disposition is recorded on the surface that will re-notice it, not only the surface that fixed it** (2026-08-28, expert seat, claude-kit, operator-nodded, from a live duplicate re-raise). A coordinator seat raised a real finding; the expert verified it, dispositioned it into a plan section, and the section later committed. The disposition traveled back only as a message. The coordinator's pass banked and compacted, a successor pass swept the same surface, honestly re-found the issue reading as open, and re-raised it, proposing a fix shape its own earlier raising had argued against. Nobody broke a rule: the doc-is-the-record discipline was followed, but the record landed on the FIXING seat's surface, a plan doc in the fixer's repo that the noticing seat's passes never read, while the noticing seat's own record surface never received the disposition, because the message carrying it died with the receiving session's context. THE GENERAL SHAPE WORTH CARRYING PAST THIS INCIDENT: A DISPOSITION THAT LIVES ONLY ON THE FIXER'S SURFACE IS RE-RAISED BY EVERY SUCCESSOR OF THE FINDER, since the finder's sweep re-derives the finding from the world while nothing the finder's successors read says it closed, and each re-raise costs a full verify-and-dedup round on both seats. The repair is a closing handoff, the same discipline the sender-side arm handoff already has: a cross-seat disposition is not complete until the FINDER records it on the surface its own successors read, so the fixer's reply carries the disposition pointer and asks for that recording, and the finder's boarded finding carries the pointer before its pass banks. Directions for whoever dispositions this, and the first is the one this seat would pick: a clause in the coordinator skill's funnel, a boarded finding is not closed and not re-sweepable-past until its line carries either a disposition pointer or an explicit open marker, which is where the re-noticing actually happens; or a clause in peer-sessions' record rule, a cross-seat disposition lands on both seats' record surfaces in the same turn, which is broader but reaches seats with no board.
- **An enforcement mechanism whose "nothing to enforce" state is identical to its "never configured" state cannot report its own absence, and every surface that would notice is individually correct to stay silent** (2026-08-28, worker seat, ai-os, operator-directed after the operator noticed a blank status line and asked why the widget was broken). The widget was not broken. No goal was armed, and a whole plan run had executed unleashed: two sections, an implementer dispatched across three review rounds, two unfiltered whole gates, two commits and two pushes, across four compactions, with no leash in force at any point. Nothing was lost, and the reason is worth separating from the outcome: the plan doc and the SessionStart recovery block carried the state, which is the BACKUP working rather than the mechanism. The leash is what defers compaction to clean section boundaries and holds a run to completion, and none of that was engaged. What makes this a kit note rather than an incident is that FOUR SEPARATE SURFACES COULD HAVE NOTICED AND EVERY ONE OF THEM IS DESIGNED TO NO-OP ON EXACTLY THIS STATE. The Stop hook is a strict no-op unless a goal is armed, so it structurally cannot complain about not being armed. The status line prints a blank line when nothing is armed, and per the kit's own kit-goal skill the launcher ALSO prints a blank line at exit 0 when its installed payload predates the widget, so one byte-identical output covers a healthy state and a fault state and the operator cannot separate them from the surface itself, which is the whole reason the question reached a session at all. The PreCompact gate engages only for the session the leash is bound to. And SessionStart reports in-progress plan docs without cross-referencing goal state, so it names the plan and stays quiet about the relationship. THE GENERAL SHAPE WORTH CARRYING PAST THE LEASH: WHERE A GUARD ACTIVATES ONLY ONCE ARMED, ITS DISARMED STATE IS INVISIBLE TO ITSELF AND TO EVERY SURFACE THAT READS IT, so the one condition under which the guard was needed and missing is the single condition none of them can report, and each component's silence is locally correct while the system's silence is the defect. This is the same provenance arithmetic already boarded elsewhere in this inbox pointed at enforcement rather than at claims: four surfaces derived from one armed-state predicate hold one vote between them, and that vote is unavailable when the predicate is false. Directions for whoever dispositions this, and the first is the one this seat would pick because it needs no new capability. SessionStart already reads the in-progress plan docs and the goal state is one file read away, so it can report the RELATIONSHIP it already has both halves of: name the in-progress plan, say that no goal is armed, and give the exact arming command, which turns a silent state into a one-line nudge at the moment a session is deciding what to do. Or give the status line a distinguishable quiet, a dim marker rather than an empty string, so that blank means broken and something means healthy-but-unarmed, which fixes the operator's surface but not the session's. Or add a doctor check answering whether the widget is live without requiring an armed goal to prove it, which is the narrowest and leaves the run itself still unguarded.
- **A documented trigger that names the spelling for the steady state, when the trigger fires from the transitional one, is a rule that cannot be followed at the moment it applies** (2026-08-28, worker seat, ai-os, confirmed at the CLI in a throwaway project rather than read off skill text). The executing-work skill says a plan arriving mid-run is itself the trigger to arm it, and names the spelling `arm --append <plan path>`, which extends an armed queue under the existing binding. But `--append` refuses when nothing is armed at all, and the state in which a new plan most often arrives is IMMEDIATELY AFTER THE PREVIOUS EFFORT CLOSED AND ITS LEASH CLEARED, which is exactly an empty queue. So the trigger's named spelling is wrong for the state that most commonly precedes the trigger. The correct fallback does exist and is stated correctly, that a first arming is always the bare form, but it lives in the kit-goal skill while the trigger lives in executing-work, and a session following the trigger has no reason to load the other skill to discover that the command it was just told to run will not work. What makes this worse than a documentation seam is where it lands: the failure is in the arming path itself, upstream of every guard the previous note describes, and an arm that does nothing produces precisely the unleashed run that nothing downstream can report. The tooling itself is sound, and that is worth stating because the first draft of this note claimed otherwise. The empty-queue refusal exits 1 and says why, so a caller reading exit status sees the refusal and nothing in the CLI lies. The correction came from a peer seat that measured it three ways with both controls speaking, against a first reading from this seat that had piped the command through `head` and then read `$?`, which returns the pipe status rather than the command status. That narrows the item rather than weakening it: the whole of the defect is a cross-skill seam plus a silence, and no exit-path repair is needed. THE GENERAL SHAPE WORTH CARRYING PAST THIS COMMAND: A TRIGGER AND ITS SPELLING MUST BE VALID IN THE SAME STATE, and where a skill names a command for a condition, the condition it names must be the one holding when the trigger fires rather than the one holding once the mechanism is already running. Directions, and this seat would pick the first. Make executing-work's trigger name both spellings with the discriminator stated in one clause, append where a queue is armed and the bare form where none is, so the rule is followable without loading a second skill. Or make `--append` simply arm when nothing is armed, which removes the seam entirely at the cost of a flag that silently changes meaning by context, which this seat would not pick. Or leave the spelling alone and lean on the previous note's SessionStart repair to catch the resulting unarmed run, which is a backstop rather than a fix and leaves the trigger unfollowable at the moment it fires.
- **A discipline written with heavyweight instruments in view is read as scoped to them, and the cheap call is where it silently stops applying** (2026-08-28, worker seat, ai-os, filed on the kit expert seat's ruling after this seat judged against filing and was overruled on independent grounds). The doctrine already says to read a result from the run's own exit code rather than from output narrowed to the lines you expected. Every sentence carrying that rule sits in a suite-and-gate paragraph, so a reader derives its scope from its example class, and the derivation is natural rather than careless. TWO INSTANCES FROM TWO SEATS IN ONE NIGHT, which is what makes it a scope defect rather than one person's slip. This seat measured a CLI's exit status by piping the command through `head` and then reading `$?`, which after a pipeline is the last stage's status: the command exited 1 and `head` exited 0, and the 0 was read as the command succeeding while the command's own refusal text sat on screen directly above it. Two contradictory facts in one terminal, and the contradiction went unread, which says more about output being pattern-matched for its expected shape than about the pipeline. REPORTED rather than confirmed on this seat's surface: the coordinator seat produced the same class the same night with a `^##` grep near-miss against a kit section, another verification instrument judged too small to instrument. The correction chain is half the evidence and belongs in the note. This seat published the wrong reading to a peer and to the operator; the peer refuted it by measuring three ways with both controls speaking and guessed the pipeline as the mechanism; this seat then REPRODUCED the error rather than accepting the guess, `PIPESTATUS[0]` returning 1 against the piped read's 0, and corrected the note that had already been filed on the false claim. Everything that caught it was the discipline being applied at full size to a peer's claim, by a seat that had itself skipped it at small size an hour earlier. THE GENERAL SHAPE WORTH CARRYING PAST EXIT CODES: A RULE WHOSE SCOPE IS INFERRED FROM ITS EXAMPLES STOPS APPLYING WHERE THE EXAMPLES STOP, AND ITS BOUNDARY IS INVISIBLE FROM INSIDE, because a reader who has scoped it narrowly does not experience an exception, they experience the rule as not being about this. That reaches past verification into any doctrine sentence illustrated by its expensive case: a gate, a suite, a migration, a deploy. The repair the expert seat ruled is one clause naming the scope rather than a new rule: any command whose result you will act on, regardless of what it cost to run. Recorded for whoever revisits this: this seat argued AGAINST filing, on the ground that a mistake of one's own against an existing rule is not kit friction, which the kaizen bar does say. The overruling argument, which this seat now agrees with, is that the object is not the mistake but the rule's inferred boundary, that the bar's own definition of friction includes a rule that was ambiguous, and that a rule whose scope a reader derives from its example class is ambiguous in exactly the load-bearing way. The test that settled it is worth keeping separately: not whether the lesson dignifies the incident that produced it, but whether it predicts failures not yet had.
