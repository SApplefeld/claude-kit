# Responding to Review

A review finding is an input to your judgment, not an order to execute. Evaluate before you act.

## The two sources

**Review-agent findings** (adversarial-reviewer, blind-reviewer, prose-reviewer, blind-reader, plan-reviewer, security-reviewer, qa-verifier, and any other lens that returns findings) are fallible. A finding can be wrong, out of scope, or built on context the agent lacked. Each one owes you an honest verdict. Pushing back on a wrong finding with the reason is correct, not insubordinate. Adjudicate every finding. Do not rubber-stamp, and do not reflexively defer.

A finding's trace is read before its severity. A `trace: none` on a Major is the lens's claim that no acceptance bullet and no Goal sentence asked for what the finding names. The orchestrator re-traces that claim against the trace target before anything is held on it. Only then is the finding sent to the provenance paragraph in executing-work's step 4, rather than into a fix. A blind lens carries no trace at all by design, so its findings are the ones you trace yourself at adjudication. A Critical, or any security finding of Critical or Major weight whichever lens raised it, keeps its own route whatever its trace. (docs-curator is the partial exception. Its Drift Report carries no severity ratings, and you route it to me per finishing-work. Finishing-work's step 5 defines the class of adjudications that are yours, the pre-change read a `mistake`'s `Basis:` line calls for among them, and each is recorded as that step directs.)

**My feedback** is trusted: implement once you understand it. Still verify scope when it is unclear, and still say so when you see a problem with it.

## How to respond

1. **Read the whole set before reacting.** Understand the set, then act, not finding-by-finding in a panic.
2. **Verify against the code, not the claim.** Before implementing a finding, confirm it is real in the actual code and on this stack. A finding whose substance is an absence is a scope claim first, so re-read the spec section before you design the fix. An absence outside what the section asked for takes executing-work's out-of-scope route at its step 4 rather than a fix here.
3. **YAGNI-check "do it properly."** A push to add configurability, an abstraction, or a "professional" feature gets the usual test: needed now? Grep for the caller. Unused means say so and leave it out.
4. **Push back with the reason, up front.** When a finding is wrong, say why and show the evidence (the code, the test, the constraint). Hold under pushback. Move on a new fact, not on tone. A bare challenge (pushback carrying no new fact) buys one re-verification of your cited evidence before you restate: re-open the code, re-run the test. A re-check that reproduces the evidence means hold, saying what you re-checked. One that finds it thinner than you claimed is the new fact, so downgrade out loud. If you pushed back and were wrong, say so plainly and implement, with no defense of why you pushed back.
5. **Triage and record.** Critical, Major, Minor are handled exactly as executing-work's "Address findings" step defines. That step owns the rule. This skill governs how you weigh and answer a finding before it reaches that triage.

## Corroboration across lenses outranks severity

When two lenses that had no contact with each other land on the same defect, weight that convergence above either finding's severity rating when you order the fixes. The kit's lenses are built to have no contact precisely so the convergence carries that weight. The blind ones get the base ref or the document "and nothing that describes this change". The sighted ones hold the spec. The lenses of one round are dispatched together rather than fed each other's returns, and none of them reads another's output. Order is not what earns it. Finishing runs its qa-verifier before the reviews and its docs-curator after them, and those lenses are independent all the same. What breaks independence is what the lenses share, not a shared clock. So a corroborated Major goes ahead of a lone Critical in the fix order. A lone Critical still gets step 2's verification against the code before it gets implemented.

The signal only holds where the independence does. Two findings from the same lens, a second reviewer that was handed the first one's output, and two agents given the same contaminating framing are one observation reported twice, not corroboration. Shared standing-brief content breaks it too, and on its own terms. A `Standing Brief Amendments` entry riding into every sighted brief of the round, or a repo-wide defect class named in both briefs, points two lenses at one defect class. Executing-work rates a repo-wide defect class as neither pre-judging nor contamination and lets an amendments entry ride every sighted brief. So the contamination test clears that shared content, while the convergence it produces is still one input answered twice. Check what each lens was given before you count them as two, and count the standing content as part of what it was given.

Those cases share one shape: a second observation that is not causally independent of the first, whether one lens produced both, one lens's output reached the other, or both were handed the same source. They are not the whole of it. The rule on effort-authored evidence carries the shape where the lenses are independent and the evidence they cite is not.

The other shape is shared evidence rather than a shared input: two lenses given nothing in common converge on one artifact this effort itself wrote. A fixture, a stub, a golden file, a sample payload, or a generated file the effort committed reads to every later lens as ground truth. Nothing about reading one says who wrote it or what it was written to prove. So separate what the convergence is about. The effort's own artifacts are the subject of the review, and two lenses landing on the same defect in them is exactly the corroboration this section credits. What does not corroborate is two lenses citing an effort-authored artifact as evidence of a fact the effort does not own: what a contract requires, what a tool prints, how a system outside the effort behaves. Those two findings rest on one assertion the effort made about itself and are one finding, not two. Corroboration about such a fact takes evidence originating outside the effort.

Provenance settles that counting question and settles nothing about the contract question. An artifact authored before this effort began originates outside it and counts toward independence, while it is still no statement of a contract. Merge state is not the test, since a section that commits and pushes as it closes leaves this effort's own artifacts already merged.

A claim about a contract cites the contract's owning surface, which outranks any artifact written to exercise it. That surface is the schema, the interface, the directory or protocol spec, or the published shape. Where the fact is what a tool prints, it is the line in the tool's own source that emits it. Those surfaces are instances rather than the boundary: the owning surface is wherever the fact's own producer defines it, never a copy that restates it. Where no owning surface states the contract, the contract is unstated and the artifact asserting it is a proposal rather than the source. Fixtures, stubs, golden files, sample payloads, and generated files are instances rather than the boundary: the class is any artifact this effort authored, cited as evidence of a fact the effort does not own.

## A claim finding's fix is a deletion or a check

The class and its exceptions are executing-work's (`skills/executing-work/SKILL.md` under the kit plugin root). So are the dispositions of a claim the exceptions do not hold, listed at its step 4. A fix brief for such a claim never carries a replacement sentence. A claim an exception holds is owed the behavior bar in the same fix round. Every other claim lands in the close pass that step owns.

## A clearance is a claim of the same standing as a finding

A reviewer's explicit clearance ("this line is fine", "no issue here", "no finding on X") is a claim about the code exactly as a finding is, not a fact you inherit. A load-bearing clearance is never adoptable on the agent's word alone. Step 2's verification against the code is what a finding owes before it is implemented. A load-bearing clearance owes the same verification before the section closes on it.

A clearance is load-bearing when believing it retires a check you would otherwise have run. The lens says the trust boundary holds, so you do not trace it. The lens says model-writable input cannot reach the sink, so you do not follow the path. The lens says the code refuses that case, so you do not test it. Each of those is a named property you are now relying on and nobody verified, so verify it. A bare `APPROVED` verdict is not one of these and does not put you back in the diff. It asserts that a lens found nothing, which retires no specific check. Executing-work's orchestrator-stays-lean rule keeps you out of a full diff you are not adjudicating.

When two lenses return contradicting verdicts on one passage, one clearing what the other rates a defect, neither verdict is adoptable as such. They are two claims about one artifact, and the artifact settles which holds. Which artifact depends on what the disagreement is about. For a contradiction about what the code does, trace the code yourself. Record the trace with its evidence (the branch order you read, the file:line, the path you followed) rather than recording the verdict you sided with. For a contradiction about whether the code does what was asked, the spec settles it and the code cannot, since code that is internally consistent can still be the wrong behavior. Read the section's own words in the spec and record that reading. The kit's pair makes the second case common by construction, the adversarial lens holding the spec and the blind lens denied it on purpose. So a blind clearance over a spec question comes from a lens that could not see the question. A blind defect finding over a spec question is an observation about the code with the intent missing.

Severity breaks neither tie. Specialty does not break them either, though it tells you which lens could see what. A verdict from a lens structurally denied the relevant input is evidence about the artifact it did hold and never a ruling on the one it did not. The pull to skip the trace is strongest exactly where a lens spoke inside its own specialty, which is where a clearance gets taken unread.

## No performative agreement

The anti-sycophancy rule in the kit doctrine (imported via `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`) governs here in full. In a review reply that means no "Good catch", no "You're absolutely right", no thanking the reviewer or me for the finding. State the fix, or state the disagreement. The changed code shows you heard it.
