## Goal

Two rules in the role skill assume a machine whose seats name themselves, and the personas fleet is a machine whose seats are named by a roster and supervised by a process. When this plan is done, the role skill's claim protocol names a third deleter of a heavy-process claim beside the writer and the coordinator: the supervising process that launched the claimant and has confirmed its process tree dead, deleting only a claim whose `Session:` is that child's and logging the delete on its own surface. And the takeover ritual takes the coordinator seat under the name the session carries, so a fleet's coordinating persona launched as `STEWARD` registers as this machine's coordinator with `Role: Coordinator` in its entry, the `HOSTNAME: Coordinator` form staying the default for a session that names itself. The contest guard reads that entry rather than the roster string alone. It matters because on 2026-09-20 a forced restart stranded a claim the protocol could never release, since the relaunched child wore the dead child's name and denied holding the box, and the fleet's steward ran the coordinator's reconciliation passes unregistered beside a stand-in session also writing the coordinator's board file, invisible to a guard keyed on a name it does not carry.

## Decisions taken at scoping (2026-09-20)

**The supervisor is a deleter, and the list closes at three.** The writer, the coordinator's probe-and-release, and the supervising process that launched the claimant and confirmed its tree dead. The supervisor's delete is the writer's own completion delete performed by the process that owns the writer, scoped by `Session:` exactly as the writer's is, and never on a reading short of a confirmed-dead tree. Alternatives refused: leaving the case to the liveness plan's disclaim-plus-transcript release, which frees the slot hours after the kill on the coordinator's cadence and still needs the probe; and a killed-session record for the coordinator to act on, a second file for a case with one party that knows.

**The delete becomes a verb form once the writer-side plan lands.** Until then a logged unlink is the record. The architect recommends, and `## Open Questions` puts to the operator at arming, that the writer-side plan's release verb gain a third form, `release --supervisor --session <claimant id> --stop-path <path>`, scoped by the claim's `Session:` alone and appending a tombstone naming the supervising process and the stop path, so the two plans converge rather than diverge; Section 1 step 3 amends that plan's decision 3 to say so only on the operator's yes. The coordinator's own probe-and-release runs unchanged whether or not a supervisor exists, since the claim file carries nothing that says whether one launched the claimant; the supervisor's delete may simply arrive first.

**This plan runs before the liveness and writer-side plans.** Its anchors are the claim-file section's sentences as they stand at `19e626ba`. Where either of those plans lands first, Section 1 re-finds its anchors in the landed text and the Chapter records the re-anchoring.

**The seat is taken under the name the session carries.** The `HOSTNAME: Coordinator` form stays the default for a session that names itself, and it is still what a roster reader looks for first. A session launched under a name an operator's fleet roster assigns takes the seat under that name, the entry's `Name:` records it, and the entry's `Role:` says which seat it holds. The ritual stops only where the session carries no name at all. Alternatives refused: renaming the fleet's coordinating persona to the seat form, which the operator ruled out since the fleet names its personas by function and the relay derives the Discord thread's title from the session name; and an operator-tier record naming the seat holder, more moving parts than reading the entry the ritual already writes.

**The contest guard reads the registry beside the roster.** A contest is two sessions claiming the seat, read from two places: a roster row carrying the seat's `HOSTNAME: Coordinator` name, and a registry entry carrying `Role: Coordinator` whose `Name:` matches a live roster row. Claims are counted per roster row after that join, the same name join the reconciliation pass already runs, so a session found by both sources is one claimant, and a session named in the seat form with an entry saying `Role: Coordinator` contests nothing by itself. Two rows so claiming freeze the board as today. The liveness plan, which rekeys the prune and the release on session identity, is not contradicted: this rule reads the entry to find a claimant and still leaves liveness to that plan's readings.
