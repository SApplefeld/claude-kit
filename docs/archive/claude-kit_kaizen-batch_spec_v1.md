Design decisions:

- **Sighted-only amendments line.** The amendments-in-effect line reaches the sighted lenses (adversarial, security, prose reviewers) and never the blind ones (blind-reviewer, blind-reader): their charters take the base ref or documents "and nothing else", and amendments are spec-side facts whose delivery would contaminate exactly the blindness those lenses exist for. The line's absence from a blind brief is by design and the skill text says so.
- **One owner for brief fields.** §1 prunes the charter-side field restatements to pointers at the template rather than adding the new line in seven places, which retires the 2026-08-18 backlog item recording that drift (four implementer charters plus the two reviewer charters' input restatements become pointers with only their agent-specific deltas kept inline).
- **Doctrine lines are amendments to existing bullets, not new bullets**, so no parity pins are owed (the deferral criterion: a content-carrying line fails loud when deleted). Nothing in §3 touches the `KIT-REGISTER-CORE` region or `## Before you send`, so `output-styles/kit.md` is out of scope and `test/output-style-parity.test.js` must stay green untouched.
- **Statusline fix is structural, not a bigger budget.** The measured cost is a second node spawn per refresh; the fix is loading the widget in-process, one mtime-keyed cached read for the goal state, and a stale-but-drawn fallback, with the script gaining its own test.

## Standing Brief Amendments

Binding on every section dispatched or opened after the date each entry carries, per the executing-work skill's recurrence and adoption rules.

- **A1 (2026-08-25, from round 1 over sections 2, 4 and 6).** Verify every supporting factual clause in an added sentence against its own file's neighbouring text and against the codebase before it ships. Three of round 1's seven Majors were motivating clauses whose asserted mechanism the same file's own contract, or a grep of the repo, denied: a notice that "never fires" where the file's contract line has it reporting its own expiry, a claim that an omission "goes unnoticed until finishing reads the Goal" where no such check exists, and a prescribed search key conditioned on a set name the rule's own worked examples do not carry. Where a clause names a downstream check, a timing, a set's contents, or another file's behaviour, cite in your report the file:line you confirmed it at; where you cannot confirm it, drop the clause rather than softening it.
- **A2 (2026-08-25, from round 1 over section 1).** A change that alters the size or membership of an enumerated set, or the field list of a template other documents cite, sweeps every neighbouring document stating a count of it. Before reporting DONE, search the set's or template's name together with number-words and digits across `plugins/claude-kit/`, and name in your report every counted claim you checked and its verdict.
- **A3 (2026-08-25, from rounds 3 and 5 over section 9).** A trigger, discriminator, or routing condition is checked against the state where it must stay silent before it ships, never only against the state that motivated it. Name the ordinary case in your report, cite the code or the rule that makes that case ordinary, and show the trigger does not fire there. Three Criticals in one section came from skipping that check: a stderr note made the mark of a real loss when the same note prints in the ordinary store-less-project state, and a compaction made the mark of a lost account when the kit's own gate schedules compaction to land at exactly those boundaries. A condition that fires on the designed-normal case is a standing false alarm, which trains its reader off the signal, and it reads in review exactly like a working trigger because the motivating case still satisfies it.

### 10. The skip-is-loss rule reaches its other consumers, and a torn line gets an exit
Model: fable

Appended 2026-08-25 during section 5's fourth review round, which is approval drift and is recorded as such in that section's Chapter. Section 5 gave `readUsage` a `skipped` count and wrote a general rule into its contract: a caller whose claim rests on having read the evidence whole needs the skips beside the status. `memq unstamped` honours that rule. Two things in the same file do not, and one new state has no exit.

`tierDecayCandidates` (:5722) keys `evidenceUnread` on `usage.status === 'unreadable'` alone and computes archive and summarize candidates over a sidecar whose skipped lines it never learns about, so a torn applied append hides a memory's applied evidence and nominates it for archive on a tally the scan itself half knows is false. Its own header already states the principle it is breaking, that the evidence columns read `unknown` rather than `never` because a line saying otherwise is a claim the scan cannot make. `recall`'s applied column (:4148) has the same blind spot at lower stakes. Fold the skip count into both, the scan's candidate suppression first.

The new state with no exit is a torn line itself. `usageStep` (:6484-6487) preserves an unparseable line deliberately and forever, and the memory-system skill bans hand-editing the sidecar, so a single torn append or a stamp written by a newer memq syncing in from another machine floors that tier's coverage on every future run, and for the operator tier on every project on the machine. That is the standing false alarm the sweep's own absent-listing rationale names as disqualifying. Give a reported malformed line a sanctioned exit: `decay-prune` is the natural owner, since it already rewrites the file and already reports what it preserved. Preserving by default stays right, so the exit is an explicit one rather than a change of default.

Two smaller corrections in the same code, both surfaced in the same round.
