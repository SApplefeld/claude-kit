# Coordinator follow-through: the seat dispatches, tracks and redirects, with tracked requests, operator steps and source-checked reports

Status: Complete
Commit Model: Branch-and-PR
Created: 2026-10-02

## Goal

The coordinator skill states the seat's evolved role as its default: the seat dispatches plans to workers, tracks them, and redirects work in flight, standing on the delegation model the role skill owns, with the artifact-and-ask shape kept only as the fallback for a seat whose delegation is unresolved. The Never-Tasks-Directly Rule, the Four Functions text that restates it, the board-write sentence, the ledger's commitment categories and the two outside summaries in `docs/architecture.md` and `docs/security-model.md` all say the same evolved thing, and the parity test pins the new principle in place of the old. On top of that role, three follow-through rules land in the paragraphs that already own each moment: an operator request outliving one reply is a tracked commitment with an owner, a next act and what it waits on, carried on the seat's goal tree under the persona plugin; a step only the operator can do goes up as numbered instructions, each with the exact command or act and what to report back; and a report naming a pull request or a worker step as open, merged, done or queued reads its source in the same pass. The ownership map names the persona-fleet moment and the rationale ledger carries each new claim with the old ones retired. The role skill's delegation model takes the persona plugin's wider reading on the operator's ruling: inside a worker's approved plan, a push, a deploy and a commit-model change are the worker's to take on a delegated seat's steer, an edit to permissions, settings or `CLAUDE.md` goes to the operator, and the peer-sessions blocker rule says the same. The doctrine's one sentence saying the delegation instance names no surface the stop-for-a-yes test gates moves with it, in the skill and its home mirror, since a role skill naming those acts under a doctrine still saying that would be a lower surface contradicting a higher one. The rest of the doctrine and the persona plugin's own coordinator instruction are unchanged.

## Approach

**The evolved role replaces the old rule in place, standing on authority the kit already has.** The role skill's delegation model already gives a delegated coordinator scoped direction over planning, scoping, sequencing and dispatching execution of plan sections, switched per machine by the operator's opt-in record. A persona seat holds its standing from its operator-authored launch instruction, which outranks the kit's skills under the doctrine's Which Text Governs, and not from its claim or the kit's model (the architect's ruling of 2026-10-02 on record ARCHITECT-1ca002ff-6b79-4b14-b89d-ee2d213533e9-7). The never-tasks rule was the layer saying the seat does not use that authority. So the rewrite names the authority the dispatch stands on, states what a dispatch and a redirect are, bounds them, and keeps artifact-and-ask as the undelegated fallback. Nothing new is granted: a seat that announces undelegated under `/role` writes the plan and asks, exactly as today.

What the rewritten rule says, in the coordinator skill under the heading `## Dispatch and Redirect Rule`. The bullets bind meaning and the implementer writes the prose in the skill's register. The one wording this plan binds is the board-write string quoted below.

- The seat dispatches a plan to a worker, tracks it, and redirects work in flight. A dispatch hands the plan as the artifact with the seat's direction to run it: for a persona worker, a queue entry on that worker's goal tree over a coordinator record, the entry being the whole of the queue instruction; for a kit session, the plan in the repository that owns the work with the direction to take it on as executing-work's inbound-plan paragraph states. A leash is still only the operator's typed `/kit-goal` in an interactive session, under the ruling of 2026-09-24, so the seat arms nothing and a worker it dispatches runs unleashed or under a leash the operator typed.
- A redirect is a steer to work in flight: pause, reorder the queue, drop, or resume on the operator's yes, inside the plan's stated Goal. A steer past the Goal, a decision the plan does not cover, a steer that would take more than two rounds to land, and a worker's reading that drifts from the Goal all go to the operator instead. For a persona worker the record mechanics, the one-record-per-cycle and urgent-flag rules and the counting of rounds are the plugin's coordinator instruction's own and the skill points at it. For a leashed kit session the queue is the operator's, so a redirect to it is a request to the operator. An unleashed kit session takes a redirect as scoped direction under the chain by peer message, and this runbook prices that line as it prices the status round, since the peer-sessions skill leaves to a seat's runbook any further message its function needs.
- The standing: a dispatch or redirect is scoped direction under the role skill's delegation model, and the seat states the bound it holds. The opt-in record is the hostname-keyed operator-tier memory record the role skill's `/role` resolves at claim time. A persona seat whose operator-authored launch instruction states its standing holds it from that instruction, which outranks this skill under Which Text Governs, and not from its claim or this model. On a machine with no opt-in record and no such instruction, the seat announces undelegated and falls back to writing the plan and asking the operator over a warranted channel. The warranted-channel list stays closed at three, the operator's keyboard in the session's own conversation, an operator-class or unclassed event in the account-allowlisted relay thread, and an artifact-borne authorization per dispatch-authority. An authorization the seat authored is still never the operator's warrant, and the stop-for-a-yes test and the role skill's exclusions still send the worker to the operator for the acts they name, whatever the seat directs.
