## Goal

A section's first review round runs its whole roster one tier above the writer, as today. Every later round of that section runs one lens at the writer's own tier. A Critical breaks the decay: a round that returns one puts the next round back at round 1's roster and tier, and decay resumes after a full-strength round that returns none. The rule lives in executing-work's review step as one paragraph and five table rows, and the two pointers to it, in the brainstorming and finishing-work skills, stay true. This is stage 2 of the lean-kit program, carrying its decision 6, "Cheaper later rounds", into the reviewer dispatch ladder, and it exists because the program measured review rounds per section rising from about one to over four while every later round re-ran three Fable lenses over a fix delta of a few lines.

## Approach

**What the rule says, and why each clause is there.** Round 1 keeps the roster executing-work already states: the code pair, or the document pair where an `Audience:` line summons it, plus the security-reviewer where its trigger holds. The security lens stays on its trigger rather than joining every round 1, by the operator's ruling above: he values it for egregious flaws in well-designed code and finds that run globally it grows edge cases and hardening past what is practical. A later round is one sighted dispatch at the writer's tier, the adversarial-reviewer where the round's fix delta touched code and the prose-reviewer where it touched documents alone, because a later round exists to re-check owed findings against the spec's trace target, which is the sighted lens's job, while the blind lens earns its place on round 1's intent-free read of a whole diff and a fix delta gives it little to read. The security lens joins no decayed round, a re-raised round running round 1's roster: a fix delta that meets its trigger still owes its round under step 4's fix-delta bar, the one lens reads it, and the finishing security pass, which runs over the whole changeset anyway, is its security read. A Critical re-raises the round after it to round 1's shape, and a re-raised round that returns no Critical hands the round after it back to one lens, so the decay is a default and never a cap on what a real defect can summon. A later round over a haiku writer runs at sonnet, since step 1's escalation fires only on a Critical or a second NEEDS_CONTEXT and no reviewer charter is written for the haiku tier.

**Where the rule lives.** Executing-work step 3 owns the review roster, the reviewer-model rule and the effort table (ownership map row: "The review roster for a section"), so the paragraph and the rows land there and nowhere else. The brainstorming skill's tier paragraph and the finishing-work skill's round-unit sentence point at the rule, and each gains one clause so its pointer stays true.
