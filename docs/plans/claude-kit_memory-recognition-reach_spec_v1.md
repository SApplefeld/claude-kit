## Decisions

Decided 2026-09-02 by the operator at the keyboard, on the Expert seat's recommendation.

1. **The recall step folds into takeover step 4 rather than becoming a step of its own.** Steps 4, 5 and 7 of the ritual are pinned by number in `test/doctrine-parity.test.js` and named as "the fourth step" in the role and coordinator skills, so a new step would renumber every reference. Step 4 becomes the read of the board and of the store together, which is also the right order: the board says what the seat owes, the store says what it knows.
2. **Trigger authoring moves to the record's birth.** `add-operator` and `add-type` take a repeatable `--trigger <type>:<pattern>` and warn on stderr, naming the `triggers` verb, when a new record is written with none. The separate verb stays for later declarations. Warn, never refuse: a record with no handle is still worth keeping, and the warning is what makes the debt visible at the moment it is incurred rather than 237 records later.
3. **The backfill is a judgment pass, run at Opus, with its handle-less remainder named rather than forced.** A trigger is a claim that a moment recognizes a lesson; a wrong one fires noise on every session that meets the pattern, so a record whose lesson has no skill, agent, tool, command or error shape is listed as handle-less rather than given a trigger to make the count look complete.
4. **The semantic route stays deferred behind the archived spec's gate, reframed.** Any future Tier 2 uses the in-process embedder with no endpoint call on the synchronous boundaries, a relevance floor, and a one-line cap, and the gate's reading is what section 4 produces. Nothing in this plan builds it.

## Standing Brief Amendments

Binding on every section opened after 2026-09-02, dispatched or inline, and folded into every dispatch brief.

1. **A change to a rule, a flag set, or a refusal set carries its enumerations with it.** This plan has produced the same defect class in two consecutive sections: an amendment lands correctly and a neighbour that counts, lists, or restates the same set is left stating the pre-change rule. The instances so far are a reference table's moment list, a usage string, two architecture enumerations, a README capsule, a skill's unattended-vector count ("five of them memq itself also refuses"), a repair bullet's refusal list, a frontmatter keyword list, and a command's own output-shape header. So before a section is called done, grep the tree for every place that counts or enumerates the set the change touched, the changed file's own other paragraphs first, since the recorded mode lands inside the file the diff already opened. A count in prose is the highest-yield shape to grep for: a cardinal word or digit standing next to the noun the change adds a member to.

2. **A refusal added in one layer is checked against the other layer that was supposed to be its pair.** Where this codebase refuses a thing, it commonly refuses it twice, once in the CLI judging its own process and once in the hook judging the child, because the two evaluate their environment separately and disagree in exactly the case that matters. A section that adds a refusal to one layer states, in its report, whether the other layer screens it, and treats an unscreened half as a finding rather than as a follow-up.


3. **A sweep's scope is derived from what states the rule, and every site it finds is named whether or not it may be edited.** Amendment 1 fired at this section's first round and the second round still found four stale enumerations, in `plugins/claude-kit/hooks/hook-canary.js` and in two `docs/` paragraphs, so the defect is now the sweep's reach rather than its absence. Two blind spots produce it, and the operator-tier record `a-control-is-blind-to-its-shared-derivation-path` names both: a scope built from the change's own file list cannot see a file that states the rule while naming none of the changed tokens, which is exactly what a canary cross-referencing a total does, and a control run inside that scope can never reveal a hole in the scope it is run inside. So the scope is stated as the whole tracked tree minus its exclusions, never as a list of included paths or extensions: `git grep <pattern> HEAD` with no pathspec is the tree, and the exemptions are filtered out of the results rather than out of the input, which also leaves the exempted corpora available to serve as the control. The two forms return the same thing on the day they are written and diverge silently forever after, an inclusion list being a hand-refreshed claim about what exists while the tree is the thing itself; the operator-tier record `an-absence-checks-scope-list-excludes-what-nobody-thought-of` is where that was learned. The predicate is what carries the rule's subject, matching every file that states, counts, probes or cross-references the set rather than only those naming a changed token. And every site found is reported with its disposition, the ones a brief puts out of scope included, since a site found and unreported is indistinguishable from one never found.

### 3. The operator and type tiers are backfilled. Model: opus

A judgment pass over every operator-tier and type-tier record with no `triggers:` line (236 and 5 at authoring; re-count at the section's start with the same grep and its control). For each record, read the body and decide which moment recognizes its lesson: the skill whose procedure it corrects or extends (`skill:<name>`, by far the commonest for a ruling), the agent type it is about (`agent:`), the tool or command it guards (`tool:`, `cmd:`), or the failure text it explains (`err:`). The vocabulary here is exactly those five portable types: `glob:` is refused on a shared tier by both the `triggers` verb and the two add verbs, and the recognition surface skips a shared-tier glob for the same reason, that a glob names a path under a project root and a tier every project reads has none. The sentence this replaces admitted `glob:` and was made false by section 2, which landed the second of those two refusals. Declare with `memq triggers <name> <type>:<pattern> --operator` (or `--type`), one record at a time so a refused pattern names its record, and never by hand-editing the frontmatter. A record whose lesson has no such moment is left undeclared and listed by name in the Chapter with one clause of why, per decision 3; the list is the plan's product as much as the declarations are, since it is the population a semantic route would exist to reach.

Two bars on the declarations. A trigger must be true of the moment, not merely related to it: `skill:coordinator` on a ruling about the board is true, `tool:Bash` on a lesson about test baselines is noise on every command. And a machine-scoped record (`machine:` in its frontmatter) keeps its scoping in the record, which the pass never widens. What that scoping does at the reading surface is narrower than this section first claimed. `plugins/claude-kit/hooks/memory-recognition-nudge.js` labels rather than filters, in its own words at the `hitStillDeclared` comment: a record scoped to another box is labelled rather than filtered, and no filter anywhere reads this field. The nudge line appends a recorded-for-machine clause instead of withholding the record. memq's machine gate validates the value when it is written, so a value nobody could validate carries no label at all; it does not scope the read. The sentence this replaces said the nudge honours the scoping through that gate, which conflated the two and is false. A machine-scoped record therefore fires on every synced box with only the label to disambiguate, and section 5 carries the re-judgement that follows.

The control, run before and after: the nudge fires for the ledger ruling on a Skill call naming `coordinator`, driven through the hook with a real PreToolUse payload rather than asserted from the frontmatter, and stays silent on a Skill call naming `role`. A dry run over ten records precedes the pass and its declarations are reviewed in the Chapter before the remaining two hundred are made, because a systematic misreading of the vocabulary is cheap to catch at ten and expensive at two hundred.

The store is a synced git repository; the pass's writes land in it through the store's own sync at the next session start, and the Chapter names the store commit that carried them once it exists. This section runs after section 2 so the warning and the coverage line are in place to read the count down.

The type tier is out of this section's reach and moves to section 5. `memq triggers` resolves the type tier from the calling project's declared `Project-Type` and takes no argument naming a type, and no project on this machine declares one, so every checkout here answers "this project declares no Project-Type ... so --type has no target" and refuses. That is a tooling gap rather than a judgment one, so section 5 closes it and carries the three type-tier declarations with it.

### 5. The triggers verb can name a shared tier's type. Model: opus

Surfaced during section 3 and appended as the approval drift it is. `memq triggers` resolves the type tier from the calling project's declared `Project-Type` and offers no argument that names a type directly, while its sibling `add-type` takes the type as its first positional (`add-type <type> <name> "<description>"`). No project on this machine declares a type, so from every checkout here the flag refuses and the type tier's records cannot be declared at all. The asymmetry is the defect: one verb writing a shared tier names that tier, and the other infers it from wherever the caller happens to be standing.

`plugins/claude-kit/scripts/memq.js`: `cmdTriggers` accepts `--type <name>`, naming the tier the way `add-type`'s positional does, while the bare `--type` spelling keeps resolving from the project's own declaration so no existing caller changes. A named type with no directory under the type tier root is refused by name with nothing written, on the same footing as the verb's other refusals. The flag stays one-of with `--operator`. The grant hook's standing on the `triggers` verb does not change, the verb being withheld from an unattended worker whichever spelling names its tier, and per amendment 2 the section states in its report whether the hook screens the new spelling rather than leaving that to a follow-up.

Then the backfill this unblocks: the three live type-tier records take their triggers under the same calibration rule section 3 used, that a trigger names the moment the mistake is committed rather than the record's topic. `memory-types/shared-type/fact-0.md` and `fact-1.md` are test fixtures that leaked into the real store, carrying the bodies "body 0" and "body 1"; they are named here and left untouched, since removing them is a shared-store delete this plan never scoped.

The verb also gains a correction path, which section 3's review proved is missing and which nothing else in the store supplies. `cmdTriggers` reads only `--type` and `--operator` and rejects every other flag as an unknown option, and it merges rather than replaces, so a trigger declared wrongly cannot be narrowed, respelled or withdrawn through the CLI at all. The routes that remain are hand-editing frontmatter, which section 3 forbids precisely so that the grammar validates every write, and deleting the record, which is destructive on a synced shared tier and loses the body. That leaves 185 declarations write-only, which is the state a review of them has to be able to act on. So the verb takes `--replace`, rewriting the record's `triggers:` line to exactly the entries the invocation names rather than merging into what is already there, refusing whole with nothing written where any entry fails the same grammar, and removing the line rather than writing an empty one where a `--replace` names no entry at all. Every other bar the verb applies is unchanged, the shared-tier glob refusal among them.

Then the corrections section 3's review established, each confirmed against the hook or against a measured command population rather than carried over from the report. The 18 `err:` entries across 12 records never fire, because a failed Bash call is not marked failed in this harness: driven live, a real exit-1 call whose output carried the declared pattern produced no nudge at all. Six of those records are `err:`-only and become handle-less. `cmd:| head` sits on `append-only-docs-invert-the-first-match-rule` and `capping-an-absence-checks-output-can-invert-its-verdict` and was measured at 38 percent of all commands. `cmd:node --test` on `node-test-multi-file-ignores-a-missing-path` is the exact spelling this plan's own calibration rule names as the thing not to do. Both triggers on `python-absent-on-scott-claude` rest on a justification that inverted the record's subject, its body saying python works on this box. `cmd:api.github.com` on `github-pr-without-gh` surfaces a credential-extraction recipe on every GitHub API call, on two machines where that record's premise is false. `cmd:<<EOF` on `quoted-heredoc-collapses-backslashes` was inverted by its own repair, so it fires on unquoted heredocs and never on the quoted ones the record is about. `cmd:until` on `bash-tool-cap-kills-the-backgrounded-child` fires on the readiness wait the doctrine mandates. `agent:docs-curator` on `subagent-inherits-the-stale-session-start-git-status`, and `agent:implementer-opus` on two siblings, each name the anecdote where the record's rule is about any agent type. The case-variant pairs `cmd:cat -A` against `cmd:cat -a`, and `cmd:git show HEAD` against `cmd:git show head`, fold to one pattern and spend both nudge slots on one moment, as does `cmd:indexOf` subsuming `cmd:indexOf(`.
