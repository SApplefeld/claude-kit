## Goal

The coordination-ledger incident had a layer under the rule that fixed it: the wording that produced the 201-kilobyte board was not ambiguous in any way a review could see. The author wrote "everything a later pass must re-derive before acting on" describing the state of the watched work; the seat read the same clause as a membership test, is-this-re-derivable-elsewhere; and both readings paraphrase identically, so any review that asks what the line says passes it clean. Two structural features did the damage, and both generalize. An enumeration followed by a trailing general clause reads as a definition whose clause is the rule and whose examples are illustrations, and nothing in the form says whether the clause summarizes the examples or extends past them. And the neighboring default was one-directional: a line you cannot confidently place goes in, and nothing pushes anything out, which on a loop that never terminates guarantees drift rather than risks it.

This plan gives the kit an authoring-time check for exactly that class of text, and flips the one inward default the incident found. Scope is deliberately narrow: the check binds only gating definitions, phrases that decide what goes in or out of a bounded artifact (the shape is a category name, a colon, a list, and a trailing clause). They are rare and easy to spot, and applying the check any wider would train authors to skim it.

Three principles, from the design dialog the material records:

1. **Test a gating definition by where its boundary falls, never by its paraphrase.** Both readings of the incident line restate identically, and the exclusion lists the two sides produced were not merely disjoint but crossed: what the author named as excluded (lessons, corrections, narrative) sat inside what the watching seat admitted. One crossed member is enough on its own, since it already establishes that two readings differ where any number of disjoint samples does not. Crossing is the discriminator rather than disjointness, and the distinction is load-bearing: what a definition excludes is the unbounded complement of what it admits, so two readers who agree perfectly still draw disjoint samples from it and a test reading disjointness as divergence fires on agreement. Crossing does not have that failure, because two readers who agree classify each other's samples identically whatever samples they drew. The check therefore asks an intent-free reader for near-miss pairs, a thing the rule admits beside the nearest thing it keeps out and the feature separating them, and has the author place those members against its own reading with the deciding clause cited. The backstop for a definition that resists the authoring forms at all is what-changes: what a reader would do differently if the line were deleted; a definition that changes no behavior is decoration, and one that changes behavior the author did not intend is this bug.
2. **The return leg is performed by a reader who never held the intent.** A round trip (intent to spec to plain terms) closes clean through the performer's own misreading when the same reader runs both legs. The kit already ships the intent-free reader: the blind-reader dispatch that runs on a spec before it leaves brainstorming, given the documents and nothing about their intent.
3. **A membership default faces the cheap direction, and it is residual.** A line wrongly excluded from a bounded artifact costs one re-derivation, or one read of whatever surface its destination rule sent it to; a line wrongly included accrues until somebody rewrites the artifact, and on a loop with no end nobody does. Doubt therefore falls out, not in. What makes that safe is that the default is residual: it decides only what the artifact's other rules have not already placed, and every class the artifact is the one record of is admitted by name elsewhere, so no such line reaches the default at all. Having no other record therefore neither admits a line nor rescues one, and that is the load-bearing half, since the content the founding incident accumulated has no other record either and a rule that spared the irreplaceable would spare the whole of it. A second fork sits under the first, which kind an admitted line is, and doubt there falls the same way: to the kind that costs a re-measurement rather than the kind that lets a stale line be read as fact.

## Standing Brief Amendments

Every dispatch brief for this plan carries these, and every section or fix round opened after an amendment inherits it.

1. **A justification the brief supplies is a claim to test, never text to transcribe.** Where a brief hands you an argument for why a rule is right and asks for it in the shipped text, check that argument against the files it is about before you write it, and report it as unsound rather than writing it where it does not hold. A transcribed argument reads as compliance, so this failure is invisible to your own gate and surfaces only at review. (Adopted 2026-09-03, section 2 round 1, on the second instance of the class in this plan.)

2. **Before writing an exception to a default, name the rule that already places each member of the exception's class.** Where such a rule exists, the exception is redundant and its only live reach is the cases that rule does not cover, which is where it does its damage. A default is residual to the rules around it, so an exception written from the class's properties rather than from what the surrounding rules already place will readmit whatever else shares that property. (Adopted 2026-09-03, section 2, on the consult's ruling.)
