# Coordinator follow-through: the seat dispatches, tracks and redirects, with tracked requests, operator steps and source-checked reports

Status: Ready
Commit Model: Branch-and-PR
Created: 2026-10-02

## Goal

The coordinator skill states the seat's evolved role as its default: the seat dispatches plans to workers, tracks them, and redirects work in flight, standing on the delegation model the role skill owns, with the artifact-and-ask shape kept only as the fallback for a seat whose delegation is unresolved. The Never-Tasks-Directly Rule, the Four Functions text that restates it, the board-write sentence, the ledger's commitment categories and the two outside summaries in `docs/architecture.md` and `docs/security-model.md` all say the same evolved thing, and the parity test pins the new principle in place of the old. On top of that role, three follow-through rules land in the paragraphs that already own each moment: an operator request outliving one reply is a tracked commitment with an owner, a next act and what it waits on, carried on the seat's goal tree under the persona plugin; a step only the operator can do goes up as numbered instructions, each with the exact command or act and what to report back; and a report naming a pull request or a worker step as open, merged, done or queued reads its source in the same pass. The ownership map names the persona-fleet moment and the rationale ledger carries each new claim with the old ones retired. The doctrine, the role skill's delegation exclusions and the persona plugin's own coordinator instruction are unchanged.

## Intent

The operator said, through the coordinator persona on 2026-10-02, that the way the steward tracks requests is exactly what they want from the coordinator role, and asked whether doctrine or guidance should change so goal-tracked, task-tracked follow-through is the default. ## Approach

**The evolved role replaces the old rule in place, standing on authority the kit already has.** The role skill's delegation model already gives a delegated coordinator scoped direction over planning, scoping, sequencing and dispatching execution of plan sections, switched per machine by the operator's opt-in record, and the persona plugin switches the same thing for a persona seat by labelling each record from the seat's live claim. The never-tasks rule was the layer saying the seat does not use that authority. So the rewrite names the authority the dispatch stands on, states what a dispatch and a redirect are, bounds them, and keeps artifact-and-ask as the undelegated fallback. Nothing new is granted: a seat that announces undelegated under `/role` writes the plan and asks, exactly as today.

What the rewritten rule says, in the coordinator skill under the heading `## Dispatch and Redirect Rule`. The bullets bind meaning and the implementer writes the prose in the skill's register. The one wording this plan binds is the board-write string quoted below.

- The seat dispatches a plan to a worker, tracks it, and redirects work in flight. A dispatch hands the plan as the artifact with the seat's direction to run it: for a persona worker, a queue entry on that worker's goal tree over a coordinator record, the entry being the whole of the queue instruction; for a kit session, the plan in the repository that owns the work with the direction to take it on as executing-work's inbound-plan paragraph states. A leash is still only the operator's typed `/kit-goal` in an interactive session, under the ruling of 2026-09-24, so the seat arms nothing and a worker it dispatches runs unleashed or under a leash the operator typed.
- A redirect is a steer to work in flight: pause, reorder the queue, drop, or resume on the operator's yes, inside the plan's stated Goal. A steer past the Goal, a decision the plan does not cover, a steer that would take more than two rounds to land, and a worker's reading that drifts from the Goal all go to the operator instead. For a persona worker the record mechanics, the one-record-per-cycle and urgent-flag rules and the counting of rounds are the plugin's coordinator instruction's own and the skill points at it. For a leashed kit session the queue is the operator's, so a redirect to it is a request to the operator. An unleashed kit session takes a redirect as scoped direction under the chain by peer message, and this runbook prices that line as it prices the status round, since the peer-sessions skill leaves to a seat's runbook any further message its function needs.
- The standing: a dispatch or redirect is scoped direction under the role skill's delegation model, and the seat states the bound it holds. The opt-in record is the hostname-keyed operator-tier memory record the role skill's `/role` resolves at claim time. The live persona claim is the claim the persona plugin holds for the seat and labels each record from. The rule's standing sentence names both and says the seat is delegated where either exists. On a machine with no opt-in record and no live persona claim, the seat announces undelegated and falls back to writing the plan and asking the operator over a warranted channel. The warranted-channel list stays closed at three, the operator's keyboard in the session's own conversation, an operator-class or unclassed event in the account-allowlisted relay thread, and an artifact-borne authorization per dispatch-authority. An authorization the seat authored is still never the operator's warrant, and the stop-for-a-yes test and the role skill's exclusions still send the worker to the operator for the acts they name, whatever the seat directs.
- The Admin inbox paragraph is unchanged.
