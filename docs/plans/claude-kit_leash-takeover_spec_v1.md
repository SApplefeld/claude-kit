## Goal

When this is done, a session that starts in a project whose kit-goal leash is bound to another session reads, at session start, whether that holder is still working or has gone silent past a bound, and where it has gone silent the notice prints the one command that takes the leash over. That command, a takeover form of the arm, refuses while the holder shows a turn record inside the bound and proceeds when it shows none, rebinding the queue as it stands to the session that ran it and recording the takeover in the goal state's history with the evidence it read. A leash is therefore never taken from a session that is working, and never left stranded on one that has died or frozen for longer than the time it takes to notice. It matters because today a supervised persona relaunched after its leashed session died reads the leash as another session's business and idles until the operator notices the silence and relays a takeover by hand, which on 2026-09-16 cost the dev worker over four hours, and because sessions that freeze on a model or usage limit show a live process and a dead transcript, which is exactly the case this rule reads.

## Approach

**What exists, read from the code at 0ea175b6.** The goal state at `.kit/goal-state.json` carries `boundSession` and `boundTranscript` as a pair, written by `armGoal` (`plugins/claude-kit/hooks/kit-goal-lib.js:1895-1932`) when the arming session's id is corroborated by a transcript on disk under the harness's projects tree (`~/.claude/projects/<project>/<session-id>.jsonl`), and `armingSession` beside them as the claim route for an unbound arm. `sessionHoldsLeash` (`kit-goal-lib.js:234`) answers who holds it: the bound session where one is bound, else the arming session. The session-start hook composes the armed-goal notice in `composeGoalBlock` (`plugins/claude-kit/hooks/session-start.js:456-535`) in three voices: bound to this session, bound to another session, and bound with no session id in the payload. The second voice carries a liveness hint from `lastActivePhrase` (`kit-goal-lib.js:2457`), which reads the bound transcript's file modification time and renders it as "about N minutes ago", and it tells the session the plan is not its business and that a typed `/kit-goal <plan paths>` re-arms it. The CLI's `status` verb renders the same phrase (`kit-goal.js:395-409`). A re-arm is already a clean rebind: `armGoal` binds to whoever runs it, and the kit-goal skill names a typed `/kit-goal` as the operator's re-arm and `arm --self-armed` as a run's own, drawing its authority from the plan's committed `## Dispatch Authorization`. The Stop hook and the compaction gate both test the leash through `sessionHoldsLeash`, so a session that loses the leash while it is still running is released at its next stop rather than held.

**Why the transcript's newest turn record is the instrument, and not the file's modification time.** The kit memory store's operator tier, the shared tier every project on this machine reads, records two measurements. A heartbeat a session stamps at turn end lags by the whole length of the turn it is inside, six hours in one measured case, so it errs toward dead. A transcript file's modification time is touched by things that are not turns, fifty four minutes old on a session whose last turn was over ten hours old, so it errs toward alive. The timestamp of the newest turn record inside the transcript was accurate in both directions on every session measured. Two transcripts from 2026-09-16 on this machine fix the bound. The dev session killed that morning ran four hours of kill-performing suites and its longest silence between records was exactly ten minutes, fourteen times, each a bounded wait on a background run, because every tool call the harness runs is capped at ten minutes and the harness writes a record at each tool result. The relaunched session's one longer silence was its idle stretch with no leash and nothing to do. So a leashed session running a turn writes a record at least every ten minutes, and one silent past that in its own turns is frozen or dead. The bound is fifteen minutes: the cap plus margin. One case sits outside that reasoning and the instrument reads it. The Stop hook's clause (c) lets a leashed holder end its turn on a leading `WAITING:` line while a background dispatch runs, and the holder then writes no record of its own until the dispatch completes and re-invokes it, which for a reviewer or an implementer can exceed the bound. A running dispatch writes to its own agent transcript under the holder's session directory, so the instrument reads the newest turn record across the holder's own transcript and every subagent transcript beneath it, and a holder awaiting a long dispatch reads as alive on that evidence rather than as silent.

**The takeover is a form of the arm, and it binds nothing on its own at session start.** `arm --takeover` reads the goal state, refuses when nothing is armed or the goal is unbound, refuses when the bound session is the caller's own, reads the holder's silence across its own transcript and its subagent transcripts, refuses with the age it read when the newest record is inside the bound, and otherwise rebinds the queue as it stands to the caller. The fields it rewrites form a closed set: `boundSession` and `boundTranscript` to the caller's pair, `armingSession` to the caller's id, and `armedAt` to now. Every other field the state holds, `plan`, `queue`, `queueIndex`, `armedBy`, `condition`, `authorizations`, `history`, `blockedAdvance` and any field a later version adds, is carried over byte for byte, so the position is kept and the per-plan arming is not remade. Carrying `armedBy` over is deliberate: it is what each plan was armed under, and an operator-typed arming carries that run's parallelization request, so a takeover that rewrote it to `self` would retire that request mid-queue. The takeover continues the arming rather than making a new one. It refuses, naming the cause, when the caller has no corroborated session id, that is, when the arm would bind to nobody, since a takeover that leaves the leash unbound is the stranding this plan exists to end. It appends one entry to `history` of the form `{ kind: 'takeover', from: <old boundSession>, to: <new boundSession>, at: <ISO>, silentFor: <seconds>, instrument: <'own-transcript' | 'subagent-transcript'> }`, the instrument naming which stream carried the newest record it read. `takeoverGoal(cwd, bind, authority, expected)` takes the caller's snapshot `expected: { plan, armedAt, boundSession }` and refuses when the re-read state no longer matches it, the same compare-and-swap `advanceGoal` carries (`kit-goal-lib.js:2235-2260`) with `boundSession` added, so the CLI reads the state once, derives the silence from it, and passes that read back as the snapshot; two relaunched sessions racing for one leash then produce one holder and one refusal naming the swap. It takes `--self-armed` under the skill's existing flag rule: a session running the takeover on the notice is the run's own invocation and carries the flag, an operator typing it omits the flag, so the notice prints the flagged form. The flag records who ran the takeover, in the history entry and in `armingSession`, and does not touch `armedBy`, so it never retires the queue's arming. The session-start notice gains a fourth voice for a bound session whose newest turn record is older than the bound: it says the holder has been silent for that long, names the instrument, and prints the exact takeover command. It never runs the command. The doctrine's stop-for-a-yes list names a commit, a push and a plan doc edit as ungated and says nothing of a leash, and a primed worker is the party that decides to act on the notice, so the kit says and does not do. The fourth voice replaces the second only when the bound is crossed; inside the bound the second voice stands as it is, with its liveness hint rekeyed to the same instrument so the two voices cannot answer one transcript differently.

**The bound is one constant with one override.** `LEASH_SILENCE_BOUND_MS` in `kit-goal-lib.js`, fifteen minutes, read by the takeover and by the notice through one exported function, `holderSilence(state)`, which returns `{ silentForMs, instrument, transcript }` for the newest reading across the bound session's own transcript and its subagent transcripts, or `null` where no transcript can be read. A project overrides it with `leashSilenceBoundMs` in `.kit/goal-config.json`, a new file this plan introduces, read by the library so the CLI and the hook read one value. The file is a JSON object; keys other than that one are ignored. The value is an integer number of milliseconds between one minute and one day. An absent file takes the constant silently. A file that does not parse, is not an object, or carries a value outside that range takes the constant with one stderr line naming what was read. No environment variable, so a subagent inheriting a shell cannot shift the bound its parent reads.

**What the instrument reads and what it cannot.** `holderSilence` reads the last 256 KiB of the bound session's own transcript and of each transcript under the session directory's `subagents/` tree, its workflow subagents included, and takes the newest `timestamp` on a record whose `type` is `assistant` or `user`, the two record kinds a turn writes, across all of them; `system`, `attachment` and progress records are skipped because the harness writes some of them outside turns, and a subagent transcript whose own newest record is old contributes nothing, so only a running dispatch lifts the reading. A transcript with no such record in its tail, an unreadable transcript, or a newest timestamp more than five minutes ahead of the reader's clock returns `null`, a smaller lead being read as now to absorb clock skew, and both readers treat `null` as inside the bound: a takeover refuses on it, naming the cause, and the notice keeps the second voice. That is the fail-closed direction, because the cost of taking a live session's leash is two sessions in one tree, and the cost of not taking a dead one is the manual re-arm that exists today. The one residual the rule accepts is a frozen session that thaws after its leash moved: its next stop finds it unleashed and lets it end, so it costs at most one turn of edits, and the notice's wording says so to the session that took over.
