# Goal fit: the intent record and the add-decision

Status: In Progress
Commit Model: Branch-and-PR
Disjoint: no
Created: 2026-09-18

## Goal

A plan carries its design intent where every later reader finds it, and every decision to add something is written against that intent with its cost, so that not building is a live option at each one. When this is done, a session opening a fix round, a fresh judge ruling on a mechanism, a consultant testing a frame and a plan reviewer reading a spec all hold the same short record: what the operator asked for, what done does not need to do, what was refused and why, and what the operator ruled after the spec shipped. The record is what-and-why, never how. The design stop fires when a fix would add a mechanism that no sentence of the Goal, no clause of that record and no acceptance bullet names, at the moment the fix opens rather than two review rounds later, and the doctrine's minimum-code bullet names not building as the first option weighed.

## Intent

The problem, in the operator's words on 2026-09-18: "AI writes code that's more complex than humans would. It likes adding fallbacks, special cases, extra complexities. A human has more of an inclination to look at a solution, think 'that's too complex I don't want to do that', and either rightfully refrain from doing the feature, or figures out a more elegant way to achieve the feature." (quoted by the operator from a post he agreed with). His own framing: "the model solves for the test case in front of it. It sees only the time that it takes to write the test. That's all it ever feels. What we live with past that, together, in new model sessions, is the eternal wall clock burden that test added. The model needs to be reminded to look forward." And: "I continually circle back to the bigger picture and the goal, and ask how it fits into it. That is what guides me on whether something is really necessary or not."

What done looks like: "'Not building something' is a valid option that should be on the table, allow and/or reward refraining, don't force creation. Name the requirements, and write the costs." The design intent and its clarification "stays with me, but it gets lost after the conversation. I think we need to bring more of that into the plan docs." The record is read "at those key moments", named as "decision making when outputting code and in our review rounds, two big areas where we keep experiencing a lot of unnecessary additions and growth."

What done does not need to do: it does not price tests, which the test-requirement-axis plan owns. It does not rewrite executing-work's step 4 beyond the design stop's trigger and the add-decision, since the operator has a separate corpus read planned: "a long, sit together session where we go through each skill, look at each paragraph." It does not add a doctrine bullet: "the danger here is that we're growing prose again", and "if you see prose you can cut that contradicts this, ABSOLUTELY CUT IT."

Refused alternatives: a new doctrine bullet on long-term thinking, refused because a principle is rationalized past in one sentence and the doctrine is already the growth pattern under diagnosis. Raw transcript references as the carrier of intent, refused because a transcript is machine-local, enormous, and carries every detail the fresh reader must not hold; provenance only. Widening the test-requirement-axis plan, refused because reopening a reviewed plan to widen its premise is the creep under diagnosis. Keeping the two-round design-stop trigger beside the new one, refused in the sketch the operator approved. A dispatch-brief field naming what a section may let die, from the case study, refused because the implementer's add-decision already stops the re-homing move that field would prevent, and the brief template does not grow.

Rulings after the design conversation: 2026-09-18, the judge keeps its blindness to "the decision-making and all the nitty-gritty details that you fought through", and it holds "the bigger-picture design, the why, and goals. Otherwise, it doesn't have a frame." So the record is admissible to the scope-adjudicator and the charter's forbidden inputs otherwise stand. 2026-09-18, on the thread: a case study is illustrative, so the plan takes its lesson and never its remedy; a reviewer question adopted from it was cut on this ruling. 2026-09-18, on the thread: this record is written in the client-briefing register, the one the operator reads.

Also on 2026-09-18 the operator forwarded a case study from the ASR-CLAUDE seat, a review loop that spent ten rounds on sentences after a deletion re-homed a warning into an authentication class. Its diagnosis, in its words: "adding needs no permission and removing needs a ruling". Its remedies are not carried here, on the ruling below.

## Approach

**Where the gap is.** The kit already has the fresh judge this asks for: the `scope-adjudicator` charter rules on whether a finding or a mechanism serves the Goal, on a brief that carries the plan's Goal, acceptance bullets and Out of Scope and forbids its Approach, its Chapters, the fix narrative and the orchestrator's lean. Three things are missing around it. The judge holds the what but not the why, so a Goal too broad to refuse anything makes every fallback look like service to it. It convenes late: on a finding that traces to no bullet, or after two consecutive review rounds each leave a Major in the previous round's fix inside one mechanism, while a fix that merely adds a mechanism "takes the fix round as it does today" (`plugins/claude-kit/skills/executing-work/SKILL.md:500` on the base commit). And nothing anywhere states what an addition costs, so the session weighs the minute it takes to write against nothing.

**The record.** The spec format gains `## Intent`, after `## Goal` and before `## Approach`. It is what-and-why: the frame in the operator's words where the session has them, what done does and does not need to do, the alternatives refused, each on one line carrying its reason, and the operator's rulings after the spec ships. It is written in the client-briefing register the doctrine names for a decision ask, since this is the section the operator reads and approves, and the prose-register plan (`claude-kit_prose-register_spec_v1.md`) makes that register the one for everything a session writes. It is bounded to about 4,000 bytes, roughly one screen, read with `wc -c` over the section, and it carries a provenance line naming the session it was distilled from. It is written at brainstorming's step 9 from the design conversation while that conversation is in the window. It is admissible to the scope-adjudicator, the plan-reviewer and the consultant, on the operator's ruling recorded above, and it reaches every dispatch that holds the plan's what through the `Trace target:` line, the adversarial and security lenses included, since an Intent clause becomes a valid trace citation. A ruling made after the spec ships is appended to it the same turn, as a deliberate amendment above `## Chapters` recorded in the Chapter as drift, under the rule brainstorming already states for its Assumptions section. The charters' `## Decisions` references stay: eight parked specs under `docs/plans/` carry that section, it holds design decisions the judge is blind to by design, and the operator's ruling keeps the forbidden inputs as they are.

**The add-decision.** At executing-work's step 4, every Major entering a fix round, and at step 1 every section opening, gets one line before anything is built: what the fix changes, the Goal sentence, Intent clause or acceptance bullet it serves, whether it adds a mechanism and at what size, and what not building it costs. A mechanism is a unit of behavior that runs and that no such clause names; a fallback and a retry are members and not the boundary, and a renamed identifier, a moved line or a changed message is not one, since nothing new runs. A fix whose line adds a mechanism no clause names is a design stop, held and sent to the judge exactly as a finding tracing to no bullet is held today. So the design stop keeps its name, its seat, its buckets, its records, its fixed `BLOCKED:` lines and its Metrics count, and changes its trigger and its input: the judge rules on the mechanism proposed, from the add-decision line and the finding it answers, rather than on a mechanism built over a range of captures. The pair-counted trigger, its held unit, its restart rules, its capture range and its removal fix round are cut, since nothing has been built when the stop fires. Refuse then means the fix is written within the form the clause asks for, and the line is rewritten to say so.

**The removal finding.** A reviewer's finding that the delta built something the Intent's not-done half or a refused alternative keeps out is spec-traceable, exactly as a finding tracing to `## Out of Scope` is today, so it takes the fix round rather than the new-requirement hold. The case study's additive move is the implementer's add-decision case: a re-homed warning is a mechanism no clause names, so the status is `NEEDS_CONTEXT` rather than the move.

**The refusable frame.** A record whose not-done half refuses nothing has not done its job. The plan-reviewer gains one closed question for it, and brainstorming's self-review runs the same check before the blind read.

**The doctrine.** The minimum-code bullet sharpens in place in both pinned copies: not building it is the first option weighed, and a mechanism no requirement names is not written. The bullet grows by one sentence and this plan says so rather than calling it no growth.

**What this plan practices.** Its own `## Intent` above is the first instance of the record, written from the design conversation before section 1 exists.
