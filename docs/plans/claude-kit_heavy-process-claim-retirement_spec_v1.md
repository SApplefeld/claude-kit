## Dispatch Authorization

The operator froze the heavy-process claim rule on 2026-09-21 on the architect persona's relay thread and asked for a measurement: "Let's measure, do a test, verify, and see what happens." The measurement ran the same day under verdict rules fixed before the first round, and the verdict was to retire the claim for the suites measured. The architect put two options to the operator on that thread, retiring the claim everywhere or keeping it for suites short on memory, and recommended the second. The operator chose the first, in these words on 2026-09-21: "Option A, retire everywhere. It's clear from your test that we've addressed the CPU issues, which makes me feel enormously better." On the plan: "The Plan Sketch needs to swap to Option A, but aside from that, it's a 'Go'." And: "We may need a pass over plans to clean up things not needed as we drop the Heavy Claims references and usage."

The steward hands this plan to the kit worker persona by name, and that handoff is the assignment. The steward is the fleet's coordinator persona, the session that owns each worker persona's queue, and a handoff is this plan's filename arriving as the worker's goal from that persona. An executor checks that one thing before starting, in its own goal record, which the persona plugin's goal tools print. The rest of this section records how the authorization came about, and none of it is a precondition.

## Goal

No session writes, reads, probes or releases a heavy-process claim, because the claim file, its protocol and every instrument over it are gone from the kit. Before a suite or a build a session polls the process list and either waits on a live foreign heavy process or starts, and an overlap the poll did not see costs wall clock rather than a verdict. The sequencing rule for one repository's own build and suites is unchanged. A red seen while another suite, build or embedding pass was on the box, which then passes on a solo re-run, is contention evidence and goes to the kaizen inbox as a note stating which suite cannot share the box with what. The architect counts such notes on or after 2026-10-05 and reports the count to the operator.

## Intent

The operator's frame, in his words on 2026-09-21: "While I am inclined to cut it based on the strength of just monitoring the CPU over the last couple of days, there is the possibility that time-sensitive tests could be impacted. It could give us false spreads that cause us to just rerun a bunch of tests. Let's measure, do a test, verify, and see what happens." After the measurement: "Option A, retire everywhere. It's clear from your test that we've addressed the CPU issues, which makes me feel enormously better."

Done means the kit carries no claim file form, no claim protocol, no brief clause about a claim, no audit reading of a claim, no sync rule about a claims directory, and no test pinning any of those. It means the doctrine's box-budget bullet, its mirror and the testing-discipline bullet say what a session does before a spawn without naming a claim. It means the plans that built on the claim are abandoned or trimmed, and an overlap red has a place to show up that the architect can count. Done does not mean the process poll changes, the sequencing rule changes, the registry, the board or the Admin inbox change, or any coordination artifact other than the claim file leaves the directory contract.

Alternatives refused:
- Keep the claim for suites whose solo run leaves the box short on memory, the ai-os .NET suite today. Refused by the operator on 2026-09-21, choosing to retire everywhere. The cost he accepted is that the .NET suite runs on the poll and the sequencing rule alone.
- Keep the claim as it stands. Refused by the measurement: three overlap rounds of four node suites produced no red that passes solo, and commit headroom never fell under fifteen gigabytes.
- Retire the write and keep the read. Refused because a file nobody writes is a read of nothing, and every reader would carry dead text.
- Put a per-suite switch in each repository's test runner. Refused because nothing measured needs a switch.

Rulings after the spec shipped: none yet.

## Evidence

The measurement, read from each round's `summary.json` and suite logs on 2026-09-21. The box is a 16 gigabyte, four-processor guest carrying other worker sessions throughout.

- Baselines, each suite alone: agent_persona's sixteen offline suites exit 0 in 233 seconds; the discord-channels suite exits 0 in 47 seconds with 1850 of 1851 passing; the claude-kit suite exits 1 in 368 seconds with one failing test, `loadIndex answers a status, never a throw, for a cwd the store refuses to name`.
- Overlap, three rounds, each running two copies of the claude-kit suite, the persona suites and the relay suite at once: every count identical to the baselines, the one claude-kit failure the same test in both copies in all three rounds. Lowest commit headroom 15,467, 15,570 and 16,792 megabytes. Lowest available physical memory 6,802 megabytes. Mean processor 81 to 82 percent, run queue up to 135, up to 356 processes. Wall clock stretched: claude-kit 368 to 565-578 seconds, persona 233 to 457-470, relay 47 to 63-70.
- The one failing test is a linked-worktree defect in `sidecar/memory-index.js` and passes from a plain clone at the same commit. It is in the kaizen inbox and is not this plan's.
- A sweep over every log for `STATUS_NO_MEMORY`, `ENOMEM`, `out of memory` and the spawn-failure forms returned nothing, with a control that matched.
- The .NET suite was not measured under overlap. Its solo reading is the operator-tier record `commit-charge-ceiling`, about 5,400 processes and 2,915 megabytes of free virtual memory with that suite alone on the box, and that record already names sequencing as the remedy. Nothing in this plan changes it.
- No code writes, reads or releases a claim. `plugins/claude-kit/hooks/kit-registry-stamp.js` audits a claim file's `Started:` against the file's own modification time, its third reading. `plugins/claude-kit/doctor/install-memory-sync.ps1` refuses any `claims/` path in the sync allowlist and in `Test-MemorySyncPathAllowed`. Those are the only two code surfaces, both found by a sweep over the kit at `974bfebc` and re-read at `681688c9`.

## Decisions

1. The claim leaves whole rather than narrowing. Every surface that states the protocol, points at it, pins it or instruments it is deleted or rewritten to say nothing of a claim. A pointer left standing would point at nothing.
2. The pre-spawn rule is the poll plus the sequencing rule, and it is stated once. The doctrine's box-budget bullet keeps its class of foreign heavy process and its poll, and says that a live foreign process is a basis for waiting, that a clean poll is a basis for starting, and that an overlap the poll missed costs wall clock and is named as contention in the report. The sentence that a clean read is never a basis for starting is gone, because under this plan it is one.
3. The sync allowlist's `claims/` exclusion goes with the file, and a claim path found in a store's committed history becomes an ordinary admitted coordinator path, one `Test-MemorySyncPathAllowed` admits on the rule it applies to every coordinator path. So the doctor and the sync gate pass such a store without a new predicate. Admitting `claims/` paths is this plan's own reading of the operator's ruling of 2026-09-21 on the kaizen batch, that such a store passes both tools, declared here rather than ruled by him. A residual claim file on disk is deleted on the executing machine by section 4 and on every other machine by the operator.
4. The rationale ledger keeps its entries. The ledger's own rule keeps a retired entry so the next audit does not re-find it, so every entry whose key states a claim rule takes `verdict: retire`, the ledger's own value, with its `proposed:` line, a reason naming this plan and a `landed:` line once the commit exists, and no entry is deleted. This applies to every ledger under `plugins/claude-kit/skills/*/references/`, since the coordinator, executing-work, finishing-work, operating-instructions, peer-sessions and testing-discipline ledgers each carry entries keyed on claim rules, and each section's scope carries the ledgers of the skills it edits.
5. The tripwire is the red protocol made explicit for overlap, and it is permanent rather than dated. A red seen with another suite, build or embedding pass on the box that passes on a solo re-run is a contention finding, and it goes to the kaizen inbox as a note stating the lesson, which suite cannot share the box with what. That is the kaizen bar's own shape, so the kaizen skill is unchanged and no dated step ships in any skill. The count on or after 2026-10-05 is this plan's own verification: the architect reads such notes from the kaizen inbox and its archive and reports the count. Zero is the expected reading.
6. The plans that assumed a claim were settled in the change that shipped this plan rather than by a worker section, because two of them sat in the kit worker's queue and one would have built a dead half. So at this plan's own anchor those plans already read as follows. The coordinator-sync-machine-scope plan no longer pins the claims exclusion or the leak-screen ordering over a claims path, and says the claims directory is admitted once this plan lands. The claim-protocol-writer-side plan and the liveness plan are archived as abandoned. The fleet-coordinator-seat plan's first version is archived as superseded, and its second version in `docs/plans/` carries the seat section alone. The kaizen code batch's section 6 carries its declined-prompt line and no claim-file grading. The persona repository's dead-claim-release plan is archived as abandoned there, and its keeper-park, supervisor-gaps and goal-levels plans say a slow suite runs alone. No section of this plan touches any of them.
