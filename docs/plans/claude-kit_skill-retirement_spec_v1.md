## Goal

When this plan is done, every skill the operator has ruled unused is gone from the tree with every live reference to it, and the rules those references sat in still say what they said, or say what the operator ruled in their place. Today that is three skills: `cold` and `recap` under section 1, whose references are pointers a strip removes without touching a rule, and `park` under section 2, whose references carry rule content the operator's answer to Open Question 1 re-homes. Each ownership-map moment a retired skill owned is re-homed to the document that now states its bar or struck as a moment the kit no longer governs, the root README's payload map names no skill the tree does not hold, the parity and size-ratchet lanes are green, and the whole gate shows the same failure set as the baseline recorded at arming, which at authoring is empty.

## Approach

A skill nobody loads shapes no behavior, so its text is weight with no reader. Retirement is a deletion plus what its references need. Two kinds of reference exist and the plan treats them apart. A pointer names the skill as a place to go and leaves the rule whole when removed: the doctrine's "the `cold` skill is the dedicated tool for those verdict moments" is one, and so is the ownership map's row naming `recap`. A rule reference is a sentence whose meaning is the skill's content: the coordinator skill declares its drain window and says the steps a drained session takes "are that skill's to state", executing-work's `WAITING:` shape names "a park at a safe boundary per the park skill" as one of its two occasions and says "the park skill's drain runs first", kit-goal's leash condition (c) names the same occasion, and peer-sessions says a leashed receiver "parks on a drain line by the park skill's own steps". Strip those and five surviving rules govern the parking moment with no owner, which the doctrine's one-owner-per-moment bullet calls a defect. So the park retirement waits on the operator saying where the drain steps live once the skill is gone, and the plan carries that question with a recommendation rather than deciding it.

Where a pointer cannot be removed without changing a rule's meaning, the implementer stops and names the sentence in the Chapter rather than rewriting the rule. A test whose subject leaves the tree retires with it, per the testing-discipline skill, and that is a strip under this plan; a test that counts surfaces the deletion changes is corrected to the new count together with every prose figure that states the same count.

The three doctrine copies: `plugins/claude-kit/skills/operating-instructions/SKILL.md` is the source, `home/claude-kit-doctrine.md` is its tracked pinned copy, hand-edited identically since no build step syncs it and `test/doctrine-parity.test.js` only checks the match, and the output style's register core in `plugins/claude-kit/output-styles/kit.md` carries no Match my precision bullet and no `cold`, so the cold strip does not reach it. 1. **Where the park ritual's content lives once the park skill is gone.** The operator's. The coordinator's update window asks every live session to stop at a safe boundary, and today the surviving rules defer to the park skill for more than the four drain steps (finish or explicitly stop every live dispatch, write the interim board entry and open the checkpoint, flip the registry entry's `Status:` line to parked, reply one line the coordinator's report waits on). The question is where each of those lives, and whether the handoff file survives. Recommendation: the receiver-side content (the drain steps, the bounds, the stop-line constraint, the cancel weighing, the reply rule, the seat's wake conduct) moves to executing-work's `WAITING:` shape as the park occasion's own paragraphs, executing-work becoming row 82's owner, with coordinator, kit-goal and peer-sessions pointing there; the `.kit/parked/` handoff file is dropped with its hook inventory and `.gitignore` planting, since the registry `Status:` line and the plan doc already carry what it carried for a registered session, and an ad-hoc session's park is recorded in its own close-with-the-state message. The reason: executing-work already owns the `WAITING:` shape and its other occasion, so the content lands beside the rule that fires it, and a 5,000-word skill that was loaded once becomes a few paragraphs in a skill every run loads. The alternative, keeping the content in the coordinator skill, puts a receiver's rules in the sender's document, which is the shape the one-owner rule exists to avoid; keeping the handoff file keeps one hook block, one test and two sentences for a session shape nothing has exercised. Unanswered, section 2 waits and section 1 runs alone.
