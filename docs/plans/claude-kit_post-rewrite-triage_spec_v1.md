## Goal

When this plan is done, every item on four surfaces carries a recorded verdict against the corpus as the rewrite left it: every parked plan under `docs/plans/`, every pending kaizen note across the machine files under `kaizen/`, every active item in `docs/backlog.md`, and every kit-related memory record the store holds on this machine. Each verdict is `keep`, `re-anchor` or `retire`, in the surface-specific forms the sections name, with a one-line reason and the rewritten document and heading it was checked against. Re-anchored items are edited so their citations and quoted rule text name the new corpus. Retired items leave their surface under that surface's own rule on the operator's one batched answer, and the stored queue the program's stage 5 re-arms is regenerated from the plans that survive, in an order the operator has ruled. The suite is green with the same single permanent failure it carries today.

## Approach

The rewrite changes what the corpus says and where it says it, and every artifact written against the old corpus is now a claim about a document that may no longer exist in that form. Three kinds of rot follow, and the verdict set is built from them. A citation rots when it names a line, a heading, or a quoted sentence the rewrite moved or reworded, and the item is otherwise sound: that is `re-anchor`. A premise rots when the item asks for a rule the rewrite now states, or fixes friction the rewrite removed, or depends on a mechanism the rewrite retired: that is `retire`, and the verdict names the rewritten text that covers it. An item whose subject the rewrite never touched is judged on its own merits under its surface's ordinary bar, which for a kaizen note is the kaizen skill's pass and for a backlog item is the curating-docs skill's prune pass with its promote, retire or keep call, and is `keep` where it survives that.

The judgment is a model reading each item whole, never a script scoring it. A script may list and count, and section 1 has one do exactly that, so that every later section's verdict count reconciles against a baseline the run measured rather than a figure it remembered. The inventory carries no self-check beyond the reconciliation its acceptance states. The doctrine asks a check that reports an absence to prove it can detect a presence, and that bar is for a silence someone acts on; the inventory's silences are acted on by nobody, because every item it lists is read whole by a judge and an item it missed is caught at the reconciliation, so proving them would be effort spent on a list a stronger reader re-reads anyway.

Each judge reads the kit's own routing first rather than the whole corpus. `plugins/claude-kit/skills/operating-instructions/references/ownership-map.md` names the owning document for every moment the kit governs, so a judge takes the item, names the moment or rule it turns on, finds the owner in the map, and reads that owner in the rewritten tree. Where the item quotes a sentence, the judge searches the rewritten corpus for that sentence's subject rather than its wording, since the rewrite changed wording by design. Where the item names a mechanism, the judge reads the mechanism's skill. The rationale ledgers under `plugins/claude-kit/skills/*/references/rationale-ledger.md` carry the why of every surviving claim and are read where a verdict turns on why a rule survived.

Verdicts are files, one per item, under `.kit/scratch/post-rewrite-triage/<surface>/`, each carrying the item's identifier, the verdict, the one-line reason, the document and heading checked, and for `re-anchor` every citation as old form and new form. The files are the run's record until the Chapter and the surface edits land; they are scratch and never enter the tree.

Order. The judging is independent across the four surfaces and the surface edits are not, since sections 2, 3 and 4 all write under `docs/plans/`. So judges may overlap across sections where the box has capacity under the dispatch shape, subject to the cap of three, while every surface edit lands in section order after the operator's one answer, and section 6 runs last because it reads section 2's survivors.

### 2. Parked plans

Model: fable

One judge per parked plan, reading the plan whole against the rewritten corpus, with the ownership map as the route in. For each plan the judge writes one verdict file: `keep`, where the plan's premise and every citation still hold; `re-anchor`, where the premise holds and one or more citations name moved or reworded text, listing each old form and its new form; `retire`, where the rewritten corpus now states what the plan would build, or removed the friction it fixes, naming the covering text; or `fold`, where a surviving plan already carries the work, naming it. A plan whose subject is code the rewrite did not touch is expected to come back `keep` or `re-anchor`, and a judge that returns `retire` on one names the rewritten text that makes the code change unnecessary rather than reasoning from the plan's age.

One plan lives on a branch as well as on main. `claude-kit_coordinator-sync-machine-scope_spec_v1.md` reads `Status: Ready` on main and `Status: In Progress` on branch `coordinator-sync-machine-scope`, thirteen commits ahead of main at authoring, with its section 2 implemented and mid-review there. Its judge reads the branch copy, since that is the plan's live state, and a verdict other than `keep` on it names what happens to the branch: a `re-anchor` lands on the branch, and a `retire` or `fold` names the branch as retired with the plan so the ask's item carries both, because an archive move on main alone would leave a live branch whose merge re-creates the plan.

Every verdict other than `keep` goes to the operator in one batched decision ask, in the client-briefing register, one item per plan, each with the recommendation and what the alternatives cost, and with the `re-anchor` edits listed so he can approve them as a class. The ask's last item is the queue: a recommended order for the surviving plans, the stored order as its default, each departure from that order with its reason, and the coordinator-sync plan's open harness ruling, pending on the relay thread since 2026-09-09, folded into that item so one answer settles both. The plans move on his answer: a `re-anchor` edit corrects each rotted citation in place in the plan's body, and a dated note under the plan's header names this plan as the source and lists the pairs; a retired or folded plan moves to `docs/archive/` per the curating-docs skill with a header line naming the verdict, the covering text and the date.

Acceptance: every plan in the inventory has exactly one verdict file; every `re-anchor` lists at least one citation pair and every pair's new form resolves in the tree by content; every `retire` and `fold` names its covering text by document and heading and that text exists; the decision ask went out carrying every removal verdict from sections 2 to 5 grouped by surface and the queue item with a proposed order, and the answer, the ruled order included, is recorded under `## Chapters` with the date; no plan, note, backlog item or memory record moved before the answer.

Files in scope: the predicate is authoritative and the list below is the roster at authoring. Every `docs/plans/*.md` whose `Status:` line reads `Ready` when the run starts, other than this plan and other than `docs/plans/claude-kit_corpus-rewrite_spec_v1.md`, which is this plan's precondition and is Complete and archived before the run starts, or the run does not start. ### 3. The kaizen inbox

Model: fable

One judge per machine note file, reading every pending line against the rewritten corpus and the kaizen skill's own pass bar, as that skill reads at the run's HEAD and located by content rather than by step number, since the rewrite's own section over that skill lands before this plan runs. The pass's dispositions stand as that skill states them, at authoring apply now, promote, route elsewhere and park, and this section adds the one the rewrite creates: `covered`, where the rewritten corpus already states what the note asked for or removed the friction it named, with the covering document and heading in the verdict. A note that is `covered` is cleared from the inbox exactly as an applied note is, by its own line text and never by truncating the file, and its brief is not written. A note that is not covered takes the pass's ordinary disposition, and the pass's step 3 mechanics apply to it with one bound: the run writes the brief for an apply-now item and stops there, since this plan edits no rule text; the brief waits under `kaizen/briefs/` for the first ordinary kaizen pass after this plan closes, which applies it. The run promotes what deserves a spec into `docs/plans/` as a Ready spec, routes and parks the rest, and reconciles the staged diff's removed lines against the verdicts before the clearing commit; an apply-now line is cleared when its brief is written, as the pass clears it when the brief is applied, and the brief carries the note's text so nothing is lost between the two.

The kit's own capture rule stays open through the run: a note appended while a judge is reading is not in the inventory, survives the clear by construction, and is the next pass's.

Acceptance: every pending line in the inventory has exactly one verdict file; every `covered` verdict names its covering text and that text exists; no line was cleared before the operator's answer to section 2's ask, and every cleared line is matched to a verdict in the Chapter and no line without one was removed; the inbox files after the clear contain only lines the inventory did not carry or the verdicts left in place; briefs written are under `kaizen/briefs/` in the skill's format.

### 5. Kit memory

Model: fable

One judge over the project tier and one over the operator-tier candidates the inventory listed, each record read whole against the rewritten corpus. The doctrine's rule already binds the verdicts: a recalled memory contradicted by evidence is corrected or deleted in the same turn, updated when the fact changed, deleted when the fact no longer exists, its index line kept in step. So the verdicts are `keep`, `rewrite` where the record's fact survives but names a moved location or a reworded rule, and `delete` where the record's fact no longer exists in the rewritten corpus. Edits to the project tier go through the Edit tool on the record and its index line. Edits to the operator tier go through the `memq` CLI only, never the Write tool, per the memory-system skill: a rewrite is `memq add-operator <name> "<description>" --update --body-file <path> --confirm-shared`, and a deletion is `memq delete-operator <name> --confirm-shared`. A memory tier has no archive of its own; the store is a git repository, so a deleted record is recoverable from the store's history, and that is the reversal a `delete` verdict names.

Acceptance: every record in the inventory has exactly one verdict file; every `delete` names the corpus text or the closed plan that ended the fact and no record was deleted before the operator's answer to section 2's ask; the project memory index after the edits names exactly the records that exist; `memq recall` exits 0 after the operator-tier edits and its digest names no record the edits deleted and every record they kept; the Chapter lists every rewritten and deleted record by name.

Files in scope: the kit's project memory tier and its `MEMORY.md` index; the operator tier through the CLI only.
