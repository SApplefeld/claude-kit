## Intent

The frame, from the operator's keyboard rulings of 2026-08-31 in the KIT: Expert session: a fable dispatch should read the account rotator's own measurement before paying twenty minutes to discover an exhaustion it already recorded; the reading is re-taken at every dispatch, since the rotator moves the seat as often as every minute; and the downgrade fires only at a genuine 100 percent, since a sub-100 reading on the active seat means the rotator is still juggling. "This should probably ride behind the main queue, it's heavier."

## Goal

A fable-override dispatch under an exhausted weekly allotment discovers the exhaustion by timing out into finishing-work's never-started ladder: the five-minute first-turn window plus the five-minute probe window, then both again for the ladder's same-model retry, roughly twenty minutes per gate, re-paid at every gate in every session for as long as the exhaustion lasts, because the trigger's fact is deliberately per-gate rather than cached. On a machine running claude-swap (the operator's account rotator), the measured answer already sits on disk: claude-swap polls the usage endpoint on its own cadence and writes `~/.claude-swap-backup/cache/usage.json` (per-account five-hour, seven-day, and per-model scoped windows, Fable among them, plus a `last429At` stamp per account) beside `~/.claude-swap-backup/sequence.json` (`activeAccountNumber`). This plan gives dispatch a read-only meter over those two files, so a downgrade is recorded as measured rather than discovered, and the ladder stays live behind the gate as the backstop for everything the meter cannot see.

Four principles, settled in the design dialog and binding on every section:

1. **The reading is good only for the dispatch it was taken for.** claude-swap rebalances the active seat as often as every 60 seconds, so within a minute the machine can go from a nearly exhausted fable window to a fresh one, or the reverse. Both files are read at every fable dispatch, the account identity is never memoized, and nothing shortens the read to once per session or once per plan.
2. **Sessions never poll the usage endpoint.** The endpoint has its own request budget that cumulative polling across surfaces saturates, and a bearer token in a tool call lands in the session transcript in the clear. The meter reads claude-swap's cache and nothing else; where the cache is absent or stale, the answer is no reading, never a fetch.
3. **The reader inherits the broker's posture verbatim.** Plaintext OAuth material sits in the credentials directory beside the cache, and the cache itself carries account emails and organization identifiers. So: exactly two files read, at fixed paths under the home directory, byte-capped, with no content logged or echoed beyond the numeric fields and timestamps the verdict needs, and the reader's output pinned to carry no email, organization identifier, or claim identifier.
4. **The gate is advisory and the ladder is the backstop.** A passing meter does not guarantee the dispatch lands (a swap can move the seat mid-dispatch; the cache's usage figures are up to a poll interval old even when the seat identity is current), so the never-started ladder stays exactly as finishing-work states it, catching transient faults and everything the meter mispredicts.

## Decisions

- Decided 2026-08-31 (operator): the plan rides last on the main queue, behind board-routing-and-homing; it is heavier than the worktree batch's skill fixes.
- Decided 2026-08-31 (operator): re-check at every fable dispatch, because the 60-second rebalance means capacity measured a minute ago describes a seat that may no longer be active.
- Decided 2026-08-31 (operator, overriding the authoring draft's 90): the downgrade fires only at a genuine 100 percent, and the floor is that constant. The floor signpost the authoring draft carried was dropped at the 2026-09-24 re-anchor's plan review, as a knob the ruling does not name and a third file that principle 3's two-file rule bars. The reasoning is the operator's own: claude-swap is configured to rotate seats at 95%, so a sub-100 reading on the active seat usually means the rotator is still juggling and a swap is at most a minute away, even mid-subagent, since a swap rewrites the primary credential store and is picked up at the next request; downgrading below 100 would strand the last few percent of every seat exactly when all seats run low. The accepted exception, priced with its real cost: a long run started near-empty can exhaust mid-turn, and the weekly-wall form of that pause survives the swap (the harness never re-evaluates the resume-at-reset timer; operator-tier record `usage-pause-survives-a-cswap-account-switch`, observed on SCOTT-CLAUDE 2026-08-25 as a twelve-hour six-session freeze cleared only by hand). The operator accepts that as the rare case against the common one, and priced its frequency and magnitude in the same exchange (reported, from the operator's own meters): a large session typically moves the weekly meter only a couple of percentage points, so a run started near the wall usually still finishes inside it, and the frozen-session worst case lands roughly once per account exhaustion rather than as a recurring tail.
- Decided 2026-08-31 (expert seat, applying the ruling): `last429At` and `backoffUntil` stop being downgrade triggers and ride in the verdict line as context only. A 429 with headroom showing is the transient the one-minute autoswitch clears (operator-tier record `cswap-autoswitch-moves-the-active-account`: proactive vacating, state recorded in `autoswitch_state.json`); the ladder remains the catch for the request that bounces anyway.
- Decided at authoring (expert seat): the verdict considers the worst of the active account's scoped Fable window and its general five-hour and seven-day windows, per the note's second design catch: an account can hold fable headroom while its seven-day window is nearly exhausted, and a fable dispatch spends both.
- Decided at authoring (expert seat): the never-started-window shortening the note carries as a secondary item is out of scope; the note itself conditions it on re-measuring the first-turn tail per tier, which is a measurement task, not a section here.

`plugins/claude-kit/hooks/capacity-read.js`, a CLI with no arguments and no configuration. It reads exactly two fixed paths, `~/.claude-swap-backup/sequence.json` and `~/.claude-swap-backup/cache/usage.json`, each through `readFileBounded` from `kit-read-lib.js` at a 1 MB ceiling, touching nothing else under that directory. A `null` from the boundary, which that library returns alike for an absent path, a path that is not a regular file and an unreadable file, is the no-reading `absent file`, and a result whose `bounded` flag is set, whichever bound set it, is the no-reading `over cap`; both are decided before any parse is attempted, and the reader never parses a partial text. From `sequence.json` it takes `activeAccountNumber` only; from `usage.json` the active account's `fetchedAt`, `pollIntervalS`, `last429At`, `backoffUntil`, and `lastGood` windows (`five_hour.pct`, `seven_day.pct`, and the `scoped` entry whose `name` is `Fable`, matched case-insensitively). Nothing throws: an account that does not resolve, or a missing `five_hour`, `seven_day`, or Fable `scoped` window, or a window whose `pct` is not a finite number, is a no-reading with the fixed reason `missing window` (`unknown account` for the account), because a verdict computed over two windows when the dispatch spends three would report headroom it never measured; the context fields `last429At`, `backoffUntil`, and `pollIntervalS` are optional, and their absence changes only the context suffix or the staleness bound the next paragraph states.

The verdict, one line on stdout, in exactly one of three shapes:

- `fable capacity: scoped N%, 7d N%, 5h N% (account <n>, fetched <age>s ago) -> dispatch` where the worst window sits below the floor; where `backoffUntil` is in the future or `last429At` is within the past hour, the line carries that as appended context (`; recent 429` or `; in backoff`) and the verdict stays `-> dispatch`, per the Decisions entry demoting those signals;
- the same reading with `-> downgrade` where the worst of the three windows is at or above the floor;
- `fable capacity: no reading (<reason>) -> ladder governs` where either file is absent, unparseable, over the cap, `activeAccountNumber` resolves to no account, any of the three windows is missing, or the reading is stale, meaning `fetchedAt` older than three times the account's `pollIntervalS` (absent that field, older than 30 minutes), which is the claude-swap-not-running shape: claude-swap writes `fetchedAt` on every successful poll and keeps polling an exhausted account, so it goes stale only when claude-swap stops or cannot poll. The reason is one of a fixed set the code enumerates in one place, closed at six: `absent file`, `over cap`, `unparseable`, `unknown account`, `missing window`, `stale`.

The floor is 100, one constant in the reader, and the reader opens no third file. The output never carries an email, an organization identifier, a claim identifier, a filesystem path, or any string field from either file beyond the fixed vocabulary above and the numeric readings; the no-reading reason is from the fixed set, never an error message quoting file content, and since no path is printed the output needs none of the home-directory elision `docs/security-model.md` reserves to `kit-compact-lib.js`.
